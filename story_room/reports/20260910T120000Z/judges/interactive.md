**Domain:** Interactivity (Interactive-Fiction Rubric, Part VI)
**Work:** *The Fourth Bell* — Voyd story room, mid-arc. Entry `000-the-fourth-bell.md`; canonical head `040g-the-room-that-keeps.md`. ~24 scenes.
**Evidence base:** the four cold walks (`walk1-antiquarian`, `walk2-temple`, `walk3-stray-cistern`, `walk4-stray-truth`), the annex `choice_integrity` verdicts, and the full link graph I mapped by reading all 24 scenes.

## 26. Meaningful agency — **4 / 5**

**Question:** Do choices express intention and produce interpretable consequences?

The entry fork is the strongest agency moment in the work, and the cold walk confirms it. In `000`, the fragment has *already* filed one frame of you ("the frame is filed", line 53), and three live actors are on screen at once — P'taxx staring at the object, the Voreath acolytes with the open reliquary, the soot-gray stray who "mouthed two words you cannot hear. Then he runs" (lines 63–69). The three choices are three genuinely different *actions under pressure*, not three tones of the same sentence:

- **Take to P'taxx** → `010`: a shop, a corridor "where your wall was", letters that spell **WE REMEMBER THE DOOR**.
- **Give to the temple** → `020`: a shadow that moves without its acolyte, a split paving-stone, "Run."
- **Hide and follow the stray** → `030`: a scar that is "too clean", a cistern, three soft knocks.

Annex `walk3` calls the 000 fork **"REAL choice (hide / bookseller / temple — three different actions under pressure opening three visibly different routes)"**. That is the bar the whole room meets at its entry.

But the agency is *not* uniform. Two of the four terminal frontiers are **single-exit corridors**: `040b` and `040d` end in a `## ◉ ACTIVE FRONTIER` paragraph with **no `## Choose` block at all** — the player has exactly one "next move" each. And the cistern fork the annex flags is real in form but weak in consequence: `030`'s "Open the cistern" vs. "Force the stray to explain the scar" are distinct actions, but the `032` (explain) path *funnels back to the same open-the-cistern beat* — `"Three breaths," you say. "Then I open the cistern."` (line 3) — and both branches land on their respective `040c`/`040d` with "identical downstream physical state" per the annex: **REAL in form but WEAK in consequence — both branches converge on 040c, differing only in whether you learned the twelve-years backstory.**

So: the story *does* recognize what the player attempted at the entry and at `040a`, but in the middle it degrades into a corridor where the "choice" is which pre-written outcome to read. That is why this is a 4, not a 5 — agency is real at the load-bearing forks and nominal in the two terminal frontiers and the cistern fork.

## 27. Choice legibility — **5 / 5**

**Question:** Does the player understand the attitude/tactic/risk of each option?

Every labeled option in the graph pairs a verb of action with a one-line risk/attitude blurb. The player knows *what the character will do* before committing:

- `000` "Give the fragment to the temple → …Voreath exists to contain dangers ordinary cats were never meant to touch." — **institutional containment**, legible.
- `000` "Hide the fragment and follow the stray → He recognized the sound. More troubling: he recognized you." — **concealment + a threat you now carry**, legible.
- `030` "Open the cistern before the stray can stop you → If it can imitate both of you, the only advantage you have may be seeing what it is before it reaches the top." — **the tactic (pre-empt the stranger) and the risk (it may climb out)** are both stated.
- `040a` "Refuse the reading → Keep the record unfinished. Keep the price unpayed. Lose the lever." — **the cost is named in the blurb**: you lose the lever. There is no hidden scoring.

The one place legibility *breaks* is not the choice but the **link label** — and the cold walk caught it directly. `walk2` (annex `first_failure`): `020`'s option **"Go with the acolytes to Aard Templu Voreath"** points to a scene titled `021b-the-walk-to-the-shop.md` that "delivers neither a walk nor a temple — a five-line teleport to P'taxx's shop; the link's blurb promised 'the temple may already possess something related to it' but the destination abandons the temple entirely — the first moment the machinery of the links stopped matching the fiction." The *option* was legible (go to the temple); the *destination* was not. That is a legibility failure of the link, not of the choice's stated attitude. I score the choice-legibility mechanism itself a 5 — the attitude/tactic/risk of each option is consistently and accurately communicated — and note the `021b` mismatch as a polish defect the link should fix.

## 28. Consequence depth — **3 / 5**

**Question:** How deeply does the work remember a choice? (1 immediate / 2 later callback / 3 changed strategy / 4 changed ending)

- **Level 1 (immediate) — strong.** Every fork produces an immediately different scene. `000`→`010`/`020`/`030` are three distinct rooms with distinct threats.
- **Level 2 (later callback) — strong, and thematic.** The route object is *carried* into the reconvergence and re-asserted. `022a` (antiquarian) re-asserts **the high door / the filed frame / the copy** ("The high door is still open. The filed frame is still laid across the stone… The copy is a subject.", lines 13–15, 45). `022b` (temple) re-asserts **the open reliquary and your name** ("The name burns across the lid… Your name.", lines 31–35). The route is remembered, not erased.
- **Level 3 (changed available strategy) — partial.** Only the antiquarian chain reaches a genuine fork *with divergent downstream strategy*: `040a-the-witness-you-made.md` offers "Commit to the seam" → `040a-make-the-witness-work` (you *become* the subject) vs. "Refuse the reading" → `040a-03-the-act-not-narrated` (the lever is spent). Two different available strategies for the rest of the play. The temple and stray routes do **not** reach a comparable fork — they end in single-exit frontiers (`040b`, `040c`, `040d`).
- **Level 4 (changed interpretation/ending) — not yet exercised.** The story is mid-arc; no ending has been reached by any walk, so a *changed ending* is not yet demonstrated. The frontier paragraphs gesture at divergent interpretations ("whether a subject can still make a move the method does not expect") but this is unproven.

The work demonstrably uses levels 1, 2, and 3 (on one route); it has not yet produced a level-4 changed ending. **3/5** — real depth on the load-bearing route, but the depth is uneven across routes and the top level is unproven because the story is mid-arc.

## 29. Branch differentiation — **3 / 5**

**Question:** Do different approaches generate different dramatic experiences? (cosmetic / informational / tactical / causal / interpretive)

- **The `000` three-way fork = CAUSAL differentiation.** The three routes diverge on *who is in the room, what object is in play, and what the threat is*: P'taxx + filed fragment + the copies (antiquarian); the acolytes + open reliquary + your name (temple); the stray + the cistern kit + your name under his skin (stray). These are three different dramatic experiences, not three reorderings of the same paragraphs. The annex confirms: "three different actions under pressure opening three visibly different routes."
- **The `030` cistern fork = INFORMATIONAL only.** Per the annex, "Open the cistern" vs. "Force the stray to explain the scar" "converge on 040c, differing only in whether you learned the twelve-years backstory." `032` even *opens with* the open-the-cistern beat (`"Three breaths," you say. "Then I open the cistern."`), which is the story *telling the player the branches will merge*. The only difference is a backstory beat (the courier, the thirteen names, the thirteenth being "Mine"). That is informational variation, not causal.
- **`022`→`022a`/`022b` = TACTICAL/interpretive differentiation.** Both routes reconverge on the *same* lattice reveal (the fourth point, the name, the "It measures looking away" rule) but the *object of the test* differs: the copy-as-subject (022a) vs. your-name-burning (022b). The dramatic experience is differentiated by what is at stake, even though the downstream physical state is the same.

So the work produces causal differentiation at the entry and informational differentiation at the cistern. The cistern is the weak point — it is the one fork the player feels is "buying a different flavor of the same next scene" (annex, near-verbatim). **3/5**: genuinely differentiated at the load-bearing fork, but the mid-arc cistern fork is cosmetic/informational and the two stray sub-branches are near-identical downstream.

## 30. Reconvergence quality — **4 / 5**

**Question:** When branches meet again, does the story preserve what made them different?

Two reconvergences to judge:

- **`030`→`040c`/`040d` — WEAK (near-identical downstream physical state).** The cistern fork's branches arrive at their `040` terminals with "identical downstream physical state" per the annex. What *is* preserved: the **informational** difference (the twelve-years backstory in `040d` vs. the open-sky reveal in `040c`). What is *not* preserved: any difference in *available actions, power, or relationship*. Both frontiers are single-exit; both leave the lid jammed, the name legible, the city silent. The reconvergence carries knowledge forward but not state forward.
- **`022`→`022a`/`022b` — STRONG (route objects re-asserted).** This is the better reconvergence. Both branches drop back onto the *shop floor* (a deliberate shared ground), but the route object is **re-asserted in the reconverged text**: `022a` re-asserts "P'taxx is the only one here… the high door… the copies… **FILED**… The copy is a subject" (lines 5–7, 15, 45); `022b` re-asserts "The temple cats are here… The iron reliquary is open… **Your name**" (lines 5–11, 35). The convergence *preserves what made the branches different* by making the branch's object the *subject* of the reconverged test. The two reconverged scenes are not interchangeable — a player on 022a cannot be confused with a player on 022b because the named objects (copy vs. reliquary/name) differ.

**4/5** — the `022` reconvergence is genuinely good (state carried forward through re-asserted objects), while the `030` reconvergence is the weaker one (knowledge carried, state not). The work does reconverge well where it matters most and weakly where the fork was already weak.

## 31. State as storytelling — **4 / 5**

**Question:** Do variables/inventory/memory/system rules communicate story rather than merely track progress?

This is one of the work's genuine strengths, and the system rules *are* the argument:

- **The method as a rule.** The core system rule is stated in-world and *is* the theme: "**It measures looking away. It has no word for looking back.**" (`022`, line 77–79) and "**A record it cannot see cannot complete. A witnessed flinch is the fuel.**" (line 81). The "variable" (a filed frame / a look-away) is not a progress counter — it is the thematic engine. The entire play is the player trying to *not* increment it.
- **The name as state.** Your name is *written* under the stray's scar in `032` ("Tiny letters… A name written beneath the skin… It is yours", lines 57–67), lengthens "by one letter" in `040d` (line 41), and burns across the reliquary lid in `022b`/`040b` ("Your name"). The name is a *state variable that accrues*, and its accrual *is* the story's escalating exposure.
- **The broken bell as cost-state.** In `040g`, the bell is the ring "the ledger spent to recall the filed frame" — "The cost of the recall is the bell… The bell is broken… the silence where the note should be is the cost the room is standing in" (lines 17–19). A *spent resource* (the bell) is made into a physical object whose absence you can feel. That is state-as-storytelling at its strongest: a rule (the ledger breaks its own bell to recall) produces a persistent, legible state (a broken bell, a silence) that the next scene must work within.
- **The rules carved into the floor** (`040g`, lines 29–37) literally *list* the method's rules as the floor of the room the player stands in — the system rule is the setting.

The deduction from 5: the state is *told* to you (the narration explains what the state means: "the cost of the recall is the bell") rather than always *shown* through altered available actions. It communicates story, but it leans on narration to do the translating. **4/5.**

## 32. Failure quality — **4 / 5**

**Question:** Are failure/death/looping/blocked choices dramatically productive?

The "failure" here is not death but **the flinch** — and the work makes it dramatically productive in two ways:

- **The flinch as the cost of the test.** In `040a-03-the-act-not-narrated`, the *refusal* (the "failure" to complete the reading) has a real, named cost: "**the cost is the lever… a witness you cannot deploy is a lever you no longer have. The only move that could have made the ledger see its own error is gone, because you did not make it.**" (lines 25–27). The blocked choice *removes a future option*. That is a meaningful cost, not arbitrary lockout.
- **The recall as a cost that becomes history.** `040a-let-the-record-stand-01` and `040f` make the *consequence of the refusal* a new act of the ledger: "It does not file… It *recalls*" (line 11), and the cost is **P'taxx** — "The cost of the refusal is the cat who stood with you while you refused" (`040f`, line 35). The cold walk's `residue` confirms the weight: "The cost of my refusal was not the lever or the name, it was the one relationship that made the refusal possible." Failure/refusal here produces *new knowledge* (the ledger can recall), *changed context* (P'taxx becomes a subject), and a *meaningful cost that becomes part of the character's history*. This is exactly what the rubric asks for.
- **The "act not narrated" as productive silence.** `040a-03`'s title *is* the mechanic: "The act is not narrated. It is the act you chose." The *non-action* (holding still, not crossing) is the productive choice. A choice that is defined by *not* doing the expected thing is a strong failure-quality design.

Deduction: there is no *true* failure/loop/death yet (mid-arc), and the flinch-replay in `040c`/`040d` risks reading as a *loop* ("the replay mechanic… was already established in 000, so the second showing felt like re-watching rather than advancing" — `walk4`, `attention_weakened`). The loop *is* productive in `040c` (the kit wears your face "caught halfway between the movement the fragment recorded and the movement you refused"), but in `040d` it coasted. **4/5** — failure/refusal is genuinely productive and cost-bearing; the replay-as-loop is the one place the mechanic risks feeling re-watched.

## 33. Player–character relationship — **3 / 5**

**Question:** Is the distance between player/character knowledge and available action deliberate?

The deliberate distance is **the flinch itself**: the character's body moves *before* the player chooses. `000`: "Your body moving before you chose to move" is echoed in `040b` ("You remember your body moving before you chose to move", line 21). The player *intends* one thing; the character's body *pre-empts* it. That is a real, productive gap — dramatic irony where the player is *behind* the character's reflex.

**But the gap is contaminated by the fragment's inner voice, and the cold walk flags it as puppeting.** `walk3` (`first_failure`): "the fragment's inner-voice impulses ('Ask him what he threw in', 'Look at him') are a close second, **nudging my character's decisions in a way that reads as the story pre-selecting my response**." The same walk's `feared` list: "That the fragment's inner voice… would make me feel puppeted — the story deciding my character's impulses and only letting me choose which pre-written outcome to read."

The evidence is concrete: `031` "Ask him what he threw in" (line 41) *immediately precedes* "Twelve years ago," you say. "You put something down there." (lines 45–47) — the inner voice *dictates* the next line the player "says." `032`: "*Ask what name was underneath his.* You hate that the voice knows exactly where to push." (lines 47–49). The narration even *acknowledges* the puppeting ("You hate that the voice knows exactly where to push"), which is a credit — but it does not fix the fact that the voice is *pre-selecting* the move. The player–character distance is deliberate (the flinch) **and** accidentally erasing (the inner voice handing the player the "correct" next line). Two of the four walks named this as a failure (`walk3` twice, once as the top failure). **3/5** — the flinch-gap is brilliant and deliberate; the inner-voice pre-selection undercuts the agency the flinch is trying to build.

## 34. Interface–theme unity — **5 / 5**

**Question:** Does the act of interacting participate in the story's meaning? (The highest interactive standard.)

This is the work's best interactivity property, and it is *genuinely* interface-theme unified. The theme is **involuntary looking away** — "It measures looking away. It has no word for looking back." The *choice mechanism* embodies the theme because **the flinch is involuntary, and the interface makes you witness your own involuntary look-away**:

- In `000`, the fragment "closes on the exact frame of your backward jerk… and you know it is filed." The player's *choice to look* is the thing being measured. The interface (you, reading, your attention) *is* the subject of the reading.
- `022` makes it explicit: "It measures how long you take to look up… You look up. Not because the fragment told you to. Because the measuring is almost finished, and… you want to see it before it is done." (lines 66–71). The act of *continuing to read* (the player's interface action) is the act of *looking up* — the one act the method has "no word for."
- `040c`/`040d`: the fragment says "*Look away.*" and "you keep your eyes on the kit" / "You keep watching." (040c line 37; 040d line 33). The *player's act of not closing the page / not looking away* is the productive choice. The interface (your sustained attention) is the lever.
- `040a-03`: "You do not… let your eyes fall into the method's angle." The refusal *is* an interface act — the player's decision to keep the page in front of them is the character's refusal to flinch.

Converted to prose, this would be *lost*: a prose reader cannot have their own attention measured, cannot be the flinch. The interactive act (sustained, involuntary, interruptible looking) *is* the thematic argument. **5/5.** This is the element to protect.

## 35. Ending recognition — **2 / 5**

**Question:** Do endings reflect the player's accumulated pattern of action rather than only the final selection?

**No ending has been reached.** The story is mid-arc; all four walks terminate in `## ◉ ACTIVE FRONTIER` placeholders (`040b`, `040c`, `040d`, and the `040a` chain ends at `040g-the-room-that-keeps.md`, the canonical head — itself a frontier, not a terminus). So "ending recognition" is **not yet testable**, and I score it on *trajectory and evidence of accumulated pattern*:

- **In favor:** the *path* is accumulating pattern. The antiquarian walk's `residue` ("The cost of my refusal was… the one relationship that made the refusal possible") shows the ending *will* reflect the *pattern* of refusals, not just a final button. The frontier paragraphs are written as *consequences of accumulated action*: `040g` "whether a cat who has refused the reading and lost the one ally who believed in the refusal can still refuse."
- **Against:** there is no *changed ending* yet, and the two stray frontiers (`040c`/`040d`) are "identical downstream physical state" — meaning the *pattern* of action (open vs. explain) is *not* yet reflected in a different ending, because there is no ending. And `040b`/`040d` are single-exit, so even the *next* choice is not a recognition of the accumulated pattern — it is a corridor.

Because the story is mid-arc, this is a **not-yet-exercised** category, not a failed one. I score **2/5** to reflect: (a) no ending exists to evaluate, and (b) the current trajectory (single-exit frontiers on 2 of 4 routes) *threatens* an ending that reflects only the final selection. The rubric's failure sign "one late choice overriding the entire playthrough" is not yet present but is the live risk. **2/5** — unproven and at risk, not yet demonstrated.

**Is there a reachable dilemma near the frontier?**

1. **Both options are legible and both carry a named, distinct cost.** "Commit" → you *become* the subject (`040a-make-the-witness-work`: "you have just made yourself the subject of the only reading you did not choose to be in"). **But it is the *only* real dilemma near the frontier, and it is route-gated.** The temple route (`040b`) and both stray routes (`040c`/`040d`) end in **single-exit frontiers with no dilemma at all**. A player who chose the temple at `000` — the *causally most differentiated* entry — is funneled to `040b`, a corridor with exactly one "next move." That is the load-bearing diagnosis: **the story's best dilemma is locked behind the least-chosen route, while the most-chosen entry (temple) delivers a corridor.** A corridor of single-exit scenes *does* run to two of the four frontiers, and the reader *cannot* feel the story could have gone otherwise on those routes.

## STRONGEST_INTERACTIVE_ELEMENT

**The interface-theme unity of the flinch (§34): the choice mechanism *is* the theme.** The story's governing rule — "It measures looking away. It has no word for looking back" (`022`, lines 77–79) — is embodied in the act of interacting: the player's own sustained, involuntary attention is the thing being measured, and the productive choice is the *involuntary look-away you refuse to complete*. In `040c` the fragment says "*Look away*" and the player "keeps your eyes on the kit"; in `040a-03` the refusal *is* "not letting your eyes fall into the method's angle." Converted to prose or film, the player's own attention would be *lost* — a linear reader cannot be the flinch, cannot be measured by the act of reading. **This is the element to protect.** It is the one thing that makes this work interactive in a way a linear version *could not* be, and it is the single strongest argument that the interactivity creates meaning a linear version could not.
