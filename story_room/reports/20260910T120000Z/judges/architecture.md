## Category 1 — Dramatic proposition: **4**

The proposition is sharp and self-generating: an object that *records the involuntary flinch as evidence against the cat who carried it*, a city that has already started measuring, and a protagonist whose one stated want is to not be measured (000, final pre-choice paragraph: "You do not want to be measured. You do not want to be the subject of a reading you did not choose to be in."). That single want, plus the fragment's method (interrupt, provoke a look-away, preserve it, repeat — stated by the acolyte in 040b), generates every scene in the corpus. The story begins *now* because the fourth bell rang and the first frame was filed (000: "You watch the fragment keep it… and you know it is filed"). The entry is a true three-way fork (000: hide/bookseller/temple) — cold_player_3's choice_integrity confirms: "REAL choice… each one opens a visibly different route… Consequences actually diverge."

Deduction to 4 (not 5): the proposition's engine — "the Lattice is the city… the lattice is the one that is finishing the record about you" (022a L.43–47; 022b L.51; 040e L.57–61) — is asserted as stated fact rather than dramatized. The system's rules are *told* in 040g (the five carved rules, L.29–37) and in 040d ("A fragment is not merely evidence that the Voyd exists. It is evidence against the cat who carried it."), rather than earned by collision. The proposition is strong enough to carry the story, but its central mechanism arrives as thesis in three of the four terminals.

## Category 2 — Character objective and pressure: **4**

The objective (stay unmeasured; keep the record unfinished) organizes behavior consistently across all four walks. The pressure is real and *chosen*: the flinch is involuntary, so the only available tactic is refusal — and refusal has a price the story keeps cashing. 040a is the cleanest expression: the protagonist understands the witness "before the question finishes," weighs "You know what that costs," and then is offered a choice whose two options are each an action *under pressure with a stated price* (commit: "become the subject of the reading you did not choose"; refuse: "Lose the lever"). That is an objective exposing values under pressure, not plot instruction.

Deduction to 4: the objective is nearly static — the want never *evolves*. The arc is the same want defended at rising prices (022a L.39: "It is the same test… asked at a higher price"), not a want that bends. The pressure also leans on the fragment's inner-voice impulses ("Ask him what he threw in," 031 L.41; "Ask what name was underneath his," 032 L.47), which cold_player_3 reads as "the story pre-selecting my response." The protagonist's desire is coherent and load-bearing, but its evolution — the thing that would make category 2 a 5 — is not yet on the page.

## Category 3 — Causality: **2**

The chain *can* be traced, and at its core it is strong: square frame filed (000) → fragment files the proof (011) → lattice shows the fourth point / "it is measuring how long you take to look up" (022) → second test (040e) → witness lever (040a) → refusal spends the lever → the ledger's *recall* — "the reclassification is the consequence of your holding, not a gear that was always turning" (040a-let-the-record-stand-01 L.33) → the recall's cost is the relationship (040f) → the room that keeps (040g). Every scene on the canonical head changes the next scene's state, which is the strongest causal property in the corpus.

But the evidence also shows a serious causal-hygiene failure class, and it is load-bearing for reader trust:

1. **Route-knowledge bleed.** 031 (Stray route) says: "The Voyd. The name is not new. It is the door in the room behind the shop, seen from inside — the same ledger of looking-aways, the same filed fragment, the same countdown" (L.87). The stray route has never been to the room behind the shop; the filed fragment belongs to the Antiquarian route. cold_reader_3: "the narration suddenly assumes I have interior knowledge… that the Stray route has never given me, which breaks the first-time-player contract." This is a causal break, not a prose one: the scene's revelation *presupposes* state this route never produced.
2. **Off-route state appearing uncaused.** 021b (Temple route): the protagonist "suddenly knows 'it's measuring… counting down… to me looking up'" — cold_reader_2: "the fragment never said that on this route; the 'counting down' framing appears out of nowhere" (L.30). Also 021b L.37: the acolyte "has heard the timer before, in a different form" — knowledge never delivered to this route.
3. **Continuity contradictions.** 022b: "They do not look as though they were in the square when the bell first rang. They look as though they have been waiting for it in the dark of the shop and it finally came" (L.7) — but they *were* in the square in 020; cold_reader_2: "I could not reconcile which version of the square was canonical." Same sentence pattern in 021a L.21.
4. **Scene title/destination mismatch.** 020's link "Go with the acolytes to Aard Templu Voreath" (blurb: "the temple may already possess something related to it") lands in 021b titled "The Walk to the Shop" — a five-line teleport to P'taxx's shop that "delivers neither a walk nor a temple" (cold_reader_2 first_failure). P'taxx is inside before anyone opens the door, and "You sent him to me" is never resolved.

The failure sign the rubric names — "characters forgetting obvious solutions; outcomes determined by authorial convenience" — applies to the temple route's mid-section, and one of the four walks hit a *first failure* at this class within its first two scenes. That is why the category sits at 2 rather than 3: the causal engine is real, but the state hygiene around it is actively damaging reader trust in the causality they're being asked to follow.

## Category 4 — Escalation and increasing stakes: **4**

Stakes escalate in *nature*, not just volume — this is the arc's best structural property, and it is consistent across all four routes:

- **Scale:** personal frame → room (011) → city (022: "The lattice is the city") → the system turns to the city (040b: "So it begins asking the city"; 040d: "They know which cat to ask next").
- **Intimacy:** the cost of the refusal becomes the protagonist's closest relationship — 040f: "the recall pulled all of it back… the recall made all of it a subject. … The ledger finished the relationship." P'taxx, "the one cat in this room who was not a subject until the ledger made him one," ends with his face on the cold stone (040g L.47–49).
- **Irreversibility:** the lever is spent (040a-03: "a witness you cannot deploy is a lever you no longer have"), the recall cost the ledger its only bell (040g: "the ledger spent its only bell to tear its own record"), the cistern lid "can no longer close" (040c), the exit closes (040a-the-second-question L.63: "the stone arch that led back to the shop closes").
- **Transformed conflict:** the antagonist's method genuinely changes — from filing to *recall*, a method "the ledger has broken" (040g fifth rule). That is a new antagonist capability, not a harder version of the old one.

Diagnostic question answered: if the protagonist fails later, the loss is more intimate (the ally who made the refusal possible) and more irreversible (the bell is broken; the record is now a subject the ledger can work on) than it was earlier (a filed frame; a flinch on record). Deduction to 4: the *volume* escalation is executed by repetition — the "still X / still Y / still Z" chains in 022a (L.43–47), 040e (L.57–87), and 022b (L.49–67) all do the same dramatic job (restate the state), and all four walks flag exactly these passages as where attention goes flat ("my pulse was flat by the last line," cold_reader_2). The escalation is real; part of its delivery is sludge.

The turns land; their presentation spends the story's own best device (the carved line, the crack through FILED) on restatement.

## Causal ownership: **4 (strong at the apex, compromised at the margins)**

This is the domain where the story is most defensible. The decisive turns on the canonical head are owned by the protagonist's chosen acts:

- The held gaze in the square (000) is the frame the whole ledger works on; "You held it… with the city watching" (040e L.37, 47).
- The refusal at 040a is *the cause* of the recall: "You held your gaze. You refused the flinch. And the ledger, denied its method, did something new to finish the record it has not finished. The reclassification is the consequence of your holding, not a gear that was always turning" (040a-let-the-record-stand-01 L.31–33). This is the strongest causal-ownership line in the corpus — the system's new method is a *consequence* of the protagonist's act, which is exactly what the candidate apex law (law_bb750d1bdb10) demands, and the scene even names the mechanism explicitly so the reader cannot misread it as system fiat.
- The cistern is opened *by* the protagonist (031: "You pull anyway"), and the lid jamming is the consequence ("The stone splits under its claws. The lid drops crookedly into the crack and jams").

Where ownership is compromised:

- **Witnessing beats.** 011's filing: the fragment "slides out of the linen as though pulled by the seam… The light closes. The fragment is gone" (L.95–101) — the protagonist watches the decisive move happen. 040e: "You have not thought the words. The Lattice thinks them for you" (L.11) and the door "carves a line" (L.57) — the turn is delivered by the system, with the protagonist as witness. 022b: the name burning across the lid is the scene's entire turn, and the protagonist does nothing but see it (the acolyte's "That is impossible" is the only response).
- **Pre-scripted impulses.** The fragment's inner voice ("Ask him what he threw in," 031; "Look at him" / "Look away," 040c; "Ask what name was underneath his," 032) nudges the protagonist toward the "correct" move; cold_player_3: "they nudge my character's decisions in a way that reads as the story pre-selecting my response." This is causal ownership leaking to the system at the beat level.
- **Player → later state:** the 000 fork genuinely contributes causally (each route carries different state into 022: the filed fragment vs. the open reliquary — 022's two exit links are route-specific). The 030 fork contributes weakly: both cistern branches converge on the same "second question" terminal with the same open-lid state (040c), differing only in whether the protagonist learned the twelve-years backstory first. That is informational variation, not causal variation — and the downstream state (lid jammed, name legible, kit at the rim) is identical either way.

Net: the protagonist causes the decisive *apex* turns; the system causes several *mid-arc* turns and pre-selects impulses. That is a real, named pathology (the room's own "system narrates the next move" diagnosis), but the canonical head's most important turns are owned by the protagonist. 4, not 5, because the witnessing pattern is systemic enough to have produced first-failure-level trust damage on two of four walks.

## Category 6 — Climax and resolution: **2 (mid-arc; judged on trajectory)**

The story is explicitly in the middle of its arc (040g is an ACTIVE FRONTIER, not a terminus), so the absence of a climax is not itself a failure. Judged on what the arc has *built toward*:

- The central conflict (protagonist vs. the method that files look-aways) has been forced into decisive, physical, personal action: 040f's cost (the ally) and 040g's "the room where the refusal is a physical act, not an attitude" (frontier note) are exactly the convergence of external and internal conflict the rubric asks for. The trajectory is earned: the final room's contents (sheet = the square frame, bell = the recall cost, rules = the method) all come from accumulated story state. This is a strong setup for a climax that will require knowledge + cost + character.
- What is *not* yet there: the final choice. Every terminal is a held breath, and the frontier notes are well-posed (040g asks "whether a cat who has refused the reading and lost the one ally who believed in the refusal can still refuse"); but the arc's deepest conflict has not yet been *decided*. The 040a fork (commit vs. refuse) is the closest thing to an earned final choice, and it is mid-arc — the story has not yet paid out the cost of either branch at full scale.
- Risk to flag: the terminal scenes' "The test failed. / The file found its subject." beats (022b L.53–57) state the story's verdict *before* any decisive action, and 040e L.81–87 repeats the same verdict on the canonical head. If the climax lands on the same "test failed / subject found" formulation, the ending will read as the mid-arc thesis restated, not a consequence. The arc currently carries its own most dangerous failure sign: "ending immediately after the plot mechanism stops."

2, not 1, because the architecture *is* set up to earn a climax from its central conflict; the deduction is for the verdict-stating terminals and the unproven final choice.

Load-bearing because the next scene after the frontier must spend this word; until it does, the arc's most important residue is a promise, and the frontier note ("The next scene must address what it means to stand in the room where the method works") inherits the obligation.

## ESCALATION (summary)

Stakes change in nature, not just volume — verified on all four routes:
- **Nature:** object → room → city → the system's own method (filing → recall, "the one rule the ledger has broken," 040g L.37); the antagonist acquires a *new capability* rather than a stronger old one.
- **Personal:** the cost of failure becomes the protagonist's relationship (040f: "the cost of the refusal is the one cat in this room who was not a subject until the ledger made him one") — the ally whose presence made the refusal possible is the price of the refusal.
- **Irreversible:** every terminal carries at least one one-way door (bell broken; lever spent; lid jammed; exit closed; name legible).
- **Weakened by delivery:** the "still X / still Y" chains (022a, 022b, 040e) execute the volume escalation by repetition and are the consensus attention-kill in all four walks. The escalation is real; half of its presentation is sludge by the corpus's own anti-sludge law.

It satisfies the candidate apex law (law_bb750d1bdb10) exactly, it is the only passage none of the four walks flagged as a first failure, and it is the seed the next frontier note is built around. Protect it: the next scene must spend the broken bell and the unfinished sheet *through* this chain, not around it, and must not spend the protected image system (FILED / the seam / the bell) to re-assert route state at the reconvergence.
