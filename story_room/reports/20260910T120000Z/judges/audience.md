## CATEGORY 16 — Orientation & Controlled Uncertainty

**Score: 2 / 5**

The story is *almost* excellent here. The core mechanic is introduced with exactly the right amount of grounding: 000 establishes the "third bell / fourth bell" asymmetry, the fragment files the frame of your recoil ("the frame of your own recoil held in the black... you know it is filed"), and the rule crystallizes cleanly in 022 — *"It measures looking away. It has no word for looking back. A record it cannot see cannot complete. A witnessed flinch is the fuel."* That is mystery that withholds the *answer* while keeping the *question* legible: the audience knows precisely what is being measured and why, and still needs to know what happens to a cat that never looks away. The Stray walks confirm the question stayed alive to the end — walk3's residue is "a held breath... the right feeling for a mid-story stop," and walk2's residue is "the story never let me find out what happens to a cat that never looks away."

But two concrete orientation failures drop this below "solid":

**1. Route-knowledge bleed in 031 (the clearest orientation failure in the corpus).** walk3's `confused` entry quotes it verbatim: *"The Voyd. The name is not new. It is the door in the room behind the shop, seen from inside — the same ledger of looking-aways, the same filed fragment, the same countdown"* (031 line 87). On the Stray route the player has *never* been inside the room behind the shop, has never met the high door, and has never seen the filed fragment. The narration assumes Antiquarian-route interior state and asserts it as shared canon. This is not mystery — it is *confusion withholding the question*. The audience is being asked to care about a door they have not been shown, and the first-time-player contract breaks. This is the single most damaging orientation error because it occurs at the walk's peak-momentum beat (the kit reveal) and converts a "surprise" into a "how do I know that?" The same scene's inner-voice nudges — *"Ask him what he threw in"* (031 line 41), *"Look at him"* (040c line 7) — compound the problem by pre-selecting the character's response, which walk3 read as "the story deciding my character's impulses."

**2. The "word you have not been given a word for" placeholder.** This exact phrasing appears **four times** across 040e (lines 73 *and* 79 — twice in one scene), 040a-let-the-record-stand-01 (line 25), and 040a-make-the-witness-work (line 19). walk1 logged it as confusing twice: *"the line beneath FILED 'fills with a word you have not been given a word for'... I never get a clean sense of what the copy actually is"* and *"The 'word you have not been given a word for' phrasing landed as a placeholder rather than a meaning."* A single use of an unnameable word can be mystery. Four uses, two of them back-to-back, is a *terminology cluster* — the rubric's failure sign. The story keeps pointing at a meaning it refuses to name, and the audience is left holding a label, not a fact. (Note the irony: the story's own thesis is that the Voyd "has no word for looking back," and the prose itself falls into the same trap — it withholds a word where a word was needed.)

**3. The "copy is suddenly a subject" staging gap (walk1).** *"the copy is suddenly 'a subject'... it arrives as a stated fact rather than something I watched happen"* (022a line 45, 040e lines 71). The reclassification is *asserted* ("The copy is a subject") rather than *performed* in a way the audience can track — a hidden-basic-staging problem. The audience is told the state changed, not shown the collision that produced it.

Verdict: the audience understands enough to care (the mechanic is one of the strongest in the corpus), but two distinct failures — one structural (route bleed) and one linguistic (the placeholder) — convert controlled uncertainty into genuine confusion at precisely the moments the story wants maximum grip.

## CATEGORY 17 — Tension

**Score: 3 / 5**

Tension is *present* and *well-sourced* — the story has a genuine desire + uncertainty + consequence triangle at its best moments — but it is *routinely squandered* by the restatement chains that every walk flagged.

**Where tension works (and is earned):**
- **030's cistern build.** walk3 notes "the real pressure (the knocks from the cistern) didn't arrive until the scene's final third," and that final third is genuinely taut: three soft knocks → a voice in *your* voice ("Do not let him open it") → a voice in *his* voice → "Something begins climbing the inside wall." That is consequence approaching before the character recognizes it. The fork that follows (open now / force the truth) is a real tactical choice under a live threat.
- **032's countdown.** walk4's `attention_intensified` entry: *"the whole scene was a countdown ('One.' 'Two.' 'Three.') and that reveal landed on the final slam of the paw on the lid."* Tension delivered on a timer is the strongest form of the category.
- **040e's "Will you do it twice?"** (line 27). The test is explicitly restated as a *repeat* at a higher price ("It is the same test, asked at a higher price" — 022a line 39, 040e line 53), which raises stakes by making the consequence of failure more personal than the first time. walk1's `feared` entries show the audience was *inside* the tension: *"That the flinch would win on the second try and the fragment would file me"* and *"That refusing would cost me the one ally in the room."*

**Where tension dies — the "still X / still Y" flattening (all four walks flagged this):**
- **walk1** (Antiquarian): *"040e: the long middle chain ('ring is the sound your filed door makes' and the repeated 'still being carved / still laid / still standing' run) — the image kept circling the same four objects (door, seam, copy, line) and the repetitions blurred together."*
- **walk2** (Temple): *"022b: the long 'is' chain after 'The Lattice is waiting.'... the restatement loop drained the tension the name-burn had just built; I was re-reading the same state six times and my pulse was flat by the last line."*
- **walk4** (Stray-truth): *"040d: the middle stretch where the orange light spills across the courtyard and replays Gistli Square... the replay mechanic... was already established in 000, so the second showing felt like re-watching rather than advancing; I was coasting until 'your name lengthens by one letter' and the rooftop silence re-locked me in."*

The dramatic cause is not "repetitive" — it is **the story re-asserting a state it has already established instead of applying a counterforce to it** (see CONSATTENTION_DEATH). Tension requires *uncertainty about an approaching consequence*; a chain that restates the current state six times removes the uncertainty (the audience already knows the state) and delays the consequence (nothing new arrives), so the pulse flattens. The name-burn in 022b (the route's single best beat) is *immediately* followed by the chain that drains it — which is the worst possible placement: the tension peak is undercut by its own coda.

Verdict: the story *has* the ingredients for 4–5 tension (the 032 countdown, the 040e "twice" test, the 040f ally-loss) but spends roughly half its runtime in restatement loops that flatten the very peaks it builds. Net: solid and effective (3), held down by a single pervasive failure class.

## CATEGORY 18 — Surprise & Inevitability

**Score: 4 / 5**

This is where the story is strongest. The reveals are *genuinely* unexpected on first contact and *retroactively coherent* — they land on evidence the audience can later point to.

**The four judged reveals:**

**1. 031 — the kit is the Voyd wearing the stray's past.** walk3: *"the single most gripping beat of the whole walk."* The cistern contains not water but a night sky; a kit falls *upward* with the stray's exact scar-line and says *"You took your time."* Inevitability is earned: the stray's clean-edged ear (030 line 19, "The edge is too clean"), the black line under his fur, "mine rings first" (030 line 31), "It had me for three minutes" (030 line 65) — every one of these was planted 40 lines earlier and pays off *here*. The surprise (the stray escaped, the kit is what the Voyd learned to wear) is *causally* downstream of visible setup, not a retroactive rule.

**2. 032 — the name under the scar is *yours*, not the stray's.** walk4: *"the moment the stray parts the fur under his ruined ear and the 'scar' resolves into tiny letters that are not his name but mine — the whole scene was a countdown... and that reveal landed on the final slam of the paw on the lid."* Inevitability: the stray is a courier who "carried sealed things for cats who paid not to know my name" (032 line 13) — his whole biography is *names he delivered for others*, so the name under his skin being a *client's* name (yours) is a direct consequence of his job. The countdown structure ("One/Two/Three") forces the reveal to land on a beat, which is the craft-level definition of inevitable-surprise.

**3. 040b — the name burns across the iron lid (Temple route).** walk2: *"the first time the threat named me directly and personally, and the acolyte's whispered 'That is impossible' made the impossible feel witnessed by someone I'd started to trust."* Inevitability: the reliquary "recognized" the fragment in 020 ("The box recognized it," line 67) and its seam *matches* the fragment's seam (022b line 11), so the lid bearing your name is a logical consequence of the box having already keyed to you. The acolyte's "That is impossible" is the audience's own reaction given a voice — the surprise is *witnessed*, which deepens it.

**4. 040e — the reclassification (filed frame → subject).** The "test failed / the file found its subject" turn (040e lines 81–87) is *surprising* in direction (you *won* the test, yet the system advances) but *inevitable* in mechanism: the ledger has "no word for looking back" (022 line 79), so a cat that never looks away forces the ledger to *do something it has never done* — recall. The reclassification is the *only* move available to a system whose entire method is look-away. This is the rubric's ideal: unexpected beforehand (the audience expected refusal to be a win), coherent afterward (it follows from the system's stated constraint).

**The one soft spot (keeps this at 4, not 5):** 031's "the same ledger, same filed fragment, same countdown" (line 87) *undercuts* the inevitability of the kit reveal by importing Antiquarian-route state. The kit-as-Voyd-wearing is a perfect reveal; the sentence that explains *what the Voyd is* is contaminated by route bleed. The audience's "aha" is interrupted by "wait, which ledger? I never saw that." The *surprise* lands; the *inevitability* explanation is muddied.

**Bonus:** 040d's "A fragment is not merely evidence that the Voyd exists. It is evidence *against* the cat who carried it" (040d line 59) is a thematic surprise-inevitability pair that walk4 logged as residue that "stuck" — the fragment's function inverts from "weapon against the city" to "evidence against you," and that inversion is *caused* by the stray's dead sliver (his fragment died when he stopped answering), which is a clean causal chain the audience can reconstruct.

## CATEGORY 19 — Emotional Consequence

**Score: 4 / 5**

The 040f/040g cost is the story's most important emotional beat and it *lands* — but it lands through **specific, attached character experience, not a generic "you."** This is the rubric's "tragedy without attachment" failure sign avoided.

**Is the weight earned by attachment? Yes.** P'taxx is not a generic ally. He is built across the Antiquarian route with *specific* attachment beats:
- 010: he crosses the square *against the fleeing crowd* to catch your wrist, says "Wrap it. Do not put skin on it twice," and *"That frightens you more than the bell."* He is established as the one adult who *knows* and *protects* — the story's only consistent source of safety.
- 010: "For the first time since you have known him, he asks rather than tells. 'Which danger do you prefer?'" — a *relationship* beat, not a plot beat. He defers to you.
- 011: "You kept the wrong book," he says, "and his voice is different. Not ashamed. Tired." — he carries guilt the audience is invited to share.
- walk1's `wanted` confirms the attachment was *earned by the audience*: *"To keep P'taxx standing beside me while I did it"* and walk1's `feared`: *"That refusing would cost me the one ally in the room who had stood with me the whole time."* The audience had *wanted* his survival before it was taken — which is the precondition for the loss to hurt.

**Does it land on a specific character, not a generic "you"? Yes — the cost is *his*, dramatized through *his* specific body and look:**
- 040f line 13: *"the look is not the look of an ally. It is the look of a subject."* — the relationship is *reclassified* by the same ledger that reclassified the copy. The emotional consequence is *mechanically* identical to the plot consequence, which is thematically perfect.
- 040f line 19: *"The ledger finished the relationship."* — explicit, but dramatized through his paw pulling back: *"Not quickly. Not in fear. The way a paw pulls back from a paw it has been decided not to hold."* That is a *specific* physical action carrying the emotional weight, not an emotion label.
- 040g line 49: *"His face is on the desk... His paws are flat on the stone, his tail curled around his paws, his ears flat, his whiskers trembling. The trembling is not fear. It is the subject's look."* — the cost is *shown* in his body on the cold stone. walk1's residue confirms the audience received it as specific: *"P'taxx with his face on the cold desk, a subject now, and the silence where the bell's note should be"* and *"The cost of my refusal was not the lever or the name, it was the one relationship that made the refusal possible."*

**The one thing keeping this at 4, not 5:** the *narration* occasionally tells the emotion rather than trusting the action. 040f line 35 restates it as a thesis: *"the cost of the refusal is the one cat in this room who was not a subject until the ledger made him one."* And 040g's frontier note (line 71) explains the beat to the audience ("whether a cat who has refused the reading and lost the one ally who believed in the refusal can still refuse"). The *action* (his face on the stone, the broken bell, the silence) is doing the work; the *explanation* is redundant. The rubric's "explanations placed before the reader needs them" failure sign applies to the frontier note — it tells the audience what to feel about the loss before the audience has sat in it.

**Contrast with the other routes:** the Temple route (walk2) and both Stray routes have *no* comparable attached ally, so their endings land on *world*-scale consequence (the city widening, the rooftops going silent) rather than *personal* consequence. That is a different, valid emotional register — but it is *colder*. The Antiquarian route's P'taxx cost is the only place the story makes an event matter through a *specific character's experience*, which is exactly what category 19 asks for. The story knows this — the canonical head (040g) is on the Antiquarian route, the one that earns its emotional consequence.

## CATEGORY 20 — Momentum

**Score: 3 / 5**

Momentum is *strong* at the entry and at the reveals, and *dead* in the middle of every route. The pattern is consistent: **the story creates pressure to continue at every *turn*, and destroys it at every *restatement*.**

**Where momentum is strongest:**
- **000's three-way fork.** walk3's `choice_integrity` calls it a "REAL choice (hide / bookseller / temple - three different actions under pressure opening three visibly different routes)." The fork itself is momentum — three doors, each with a distinct *attitude* (trust the merchant / trust the institution / trust the stranger), and the audience is *pulled* into whichever they pick. The fragment filing your recoil in the opening (000 line 53) sets a *persistent* "what will it file next?" pressure that carries through every scene.
- **031's kit reveal** — walk3: "the single most gripping beat of the whole walk." The night-sky cistern + falling kit + "You took your time" is momentum as *informational shock* — the audience must continue to understand what they just saw.
- **032's countdown** — walk4: "that reveal landed on the final slam of the paw on the lid." Countdown = pure forward pressure.
- **040d's rooftop silence** — walk4: "the rooftop silence re-locked me in." "The silence is worse than the screaming" (040d line 65) — dread as momentum.

**Where momentum dies (the same failure class as 17, but framed as forward-pressure loss):**
- **022a's opening re-statement** — walk1's *first_failure*: *"the opening re-statement ('The lattice drops you back onto the shop floor / P'taxx is the only one here / There is no procession...') reads as a reset of things I already saw in 011, and by the 'The seam is still aiming. The line is still being carved. The subject is still the copy.' run the repetition starts to feel like the story is spinning its wheels rather than advancing."* This is the **earliest** momentum failure in the corpus — it happens *before* the Antiquarian route's first new beat. The scene re-asserts the state the audience already holds (the copies, the high door, FILED) and then *re-asserts it again* (lines 43 and 47, both "still" chains). Nothing new arrives for ~50 lines.
- **022b's "is" chain** — walk2: *"the restatement loop drained the tension the name-burn had just built; I was re-reading the same state six times and my pulse was flat by the last line."*
- **040e's middle** — walk1: *"the image kept circling the same four objects (door, seam, copy, line) and the repetitions blurred together."*
- **040d's replay** — walk4: *"the replay mechanic (second-you, the jerk backward, the flinch) was already established in 000, so the second showing felt like re-watching rather than advancing; I was coasting."*

**The dramatic cause (consistent across all four walks):** each of these passages **solves its own question without opening a stronger one.** The scene has already told the audience the state ("the high door is open, the line is being carved, the copy is standing"). The correct next move is to *apply a counterforce* to that state (a new beat, a new cost, a new reveal). Instead the prose *re-describes* the state, which is the rubric's "repeated information" failure sign. The audience has already *processed* the state; re-describing it gives them nothing to anticipate, so the pressure to continue drops to zero. The story then has to *re-earn* momentum with a new beat (040d's "your name lengthens by one letter," 040e's "The test failed"), which is why the walks describe "coasting until X" — the X is the first *new* fact in the passage.

**The strongest sustained momentum in the corpus** is 030→031→040c (walk3's path): the alley dialogue is slow (walk3: "competent but slow"), but the cistern sequence is a *continuous* escalation (knocks → voice in your voice → climbing thing → open lid → night sky → falling kit → "You took your time" → "it will remember being me") with *no restatement* between beats. Each beat answers the last and creates the next. This is the rubric's beat-progression ideal and the single best-run momentum sequence in the story.

## CONSATTENTION_DEATH

**The "still X / still Y / still Z" restatement chains are the one failure class all four walks agreed on.** This is not a prose quirk; it is a *dramatic* failure with a specific cause.

**The passages (quoted):**

**022a (Antiquarian), lines 43–47:**
> "The high door is **still** open. The filed frame is **still** laid across the stone. The line beneath **FILED** is **still** being carved. And the copy that was never yours is standing between you and the opened seam..."
>
> "The seam is opened. The line is being finished. The city is watching. The high door is **still** open. The copy is **still** standing. The seam is **still** aiming. The line is **still** being carved. The subject is **still** the copy. The record about you is the one that was never finished. The Lattice is the one that is finishing it."

**022b (Temple), line 51:**
> "The reliquary is **still** open. The name is **still** burning. The seam is **still** matching. And the city is watching, and the lattice is the city, and the lattice is the one that is finishing the record about you, and the record about you is the one that was never finished, and the one that was never finished is the one the Lattice is finishing now, and the name is the one the Lattice is reading, and the reading is the one the city is watching."

**040e (Antiquarian), lines 65, 77:**
> "the room is **still** open, and the high door is **still** ringing, and the filed frame is **still** laid across the stone, and the copy is **still** standing between you and the seam."
>
> "the room is **still** open, and the high door is **still** ringing, and the filed frame is **still** laid across the stone, and the copy is **still** standing between you and the seam."

**The dramatic cause (not "repetitive"):**

Each chain is a **state-reassertion where a counterforce should be.** The story's own physics (STORY_PHYSICS.md) demands three movements per dramatic unit: claim → counterforce → turn. A "still X" chain is *movement 1 repeated without movements 2 or 3*. The state ("the high door is open, the line is being carved, the copy is standing") was already established and already *processed* by the audience. Re-asserting it does not apply pressure; it *re-describes a static scene*. The dramatic function of a beat is to *change* what can happen next; a restatement *preserves* what can happen, which is the opposite of pressure.

The specific mechanism of the death:
1. **The chain removes uncertainty.** Tension (cat 17) requires the audience to *not know* the next state. "The high door is still open" tells the audience the next state *is* the current state. There is nothing to anticipate.
2. **The chain delays consequence.** The next *actual* beat (040e's "The test failed," 022b's "The test failed") is what the audience is waiting for. Each "still X" sentence is a *beat of nothing* between the audience's anticipation and the payoff. The walks describe this precisely: walk2 "my pulse was flat by the last line," walk4 "I was coasting until 'your name lengthens by one letter.'"
3. **The chain is thematically self-undermining.** The story's thesis is that the Voyd "has no word for looking back" — the entire system runs on *change* (look-away → file → recall). A prose passage that *re-describes an unchanging state for eight sentences* is the Voyd's *opposite*: pure stasis. The prose enacts the anti-theme. The audience feels the mismatch even if they can't name it.
4. **The "and" chaining compounds it.** 022b line 51 is a single sentence of *eight* "and" clauses, each re-asserting a variant of "the Lattice is finishing the record about you." That is not restatement; that is *one thought said eight times*. The audience's working memory cannot hold eight variants of the same fact and the passage collapses into noise (walk1: "the repetitions blurred together").

**The fix is structural, not linguistic.** Each of these passages should be *one* state-description (the audience already knows the state) followed *immediately* by the counterforce that changes it. 022a lines 43–47 (two "still" chains, ~100 words) could be a single sentence: "The high door is open, the line is being carved, the copy is standing between you and the seam." The next sentence should be the *test* (040e's "Will you do it twice?"), not a second description of the same state. The story already knows how to do this — 031's cistern sequence has zero restatement and is the corpus's best momentum run.

**Why this is the one agreed-upon failure:** it is the *only* failure class that appears in all four walks' `attention_weakened` entries, across all three routes (Antiquarian, Temple, both Stray). It is not a route-specific problem; it is a *systemic* problem in the Antiquarian/Temple convergence scenes (022a, 022b, 040e), which are the scenes all routes pass through. The Stray route is cleaner (031, 032, 040c, 040d have far fewer "still" chains) and the Stray walks are correspondingly less "coasting" — which is direct evidence that the chains, not the story, are the cause.

## STRONGEST_AUDIENCE_ELEMENT

**The 031 kit reveal: "You took your time."** (031, lines 17–72)

This is the single strongest audience-experience element to protect, and it is strong across *all five* categories simultaneously:

- **Orientation (16):** the night-sky cistern is *grounded* — the audience knows exactly where they are (the courtyard, the cistern, the lid), and the reveal is a *mystery* (what is the kit?) not a *confusion* (which route is this?). The only contamination is the "same ledger, same filed fragment" sentence (line 87), which is a *separate* fixable line, not a structural problem with the reveal itself.
- **Tension (17):** the build is a *continuous* escalation with zero restatement — knocks → voice in your voice → climbing thing → open lid → night sky → falling kit. The audience is *inside* the tension the entire way. walk3: "the single most gripping beat of the whole walk."
- **Surprise & inevitability (18):** the reveal is *causally* downstream of five planted details (clean-edged ear, black line, "mine rings first," "three minutes," the dead sliver) that the audience can reconstruct *afterward*. It is the rubric's ideal: unexpected before, coherent after.
- **Emotional consequence (19):** the kit says *"And if you close it, he never gets to remember being whole"* (031 line 103) — the emotional cost is *specific* (the stray's wholeness) and *immediate* (the audience must now choose between the stray's past and the city's future). The stray "staggers backward" (line 73) — a *specific* physical reaction, not an emotion label.
- **Momentum (20):** the beat *is* momentum. The audience cannot stop reading — the kit is in the cistern, the lid is open, the thing above is "moving," and the fork (040c) is a *real* choice under a live threat. walk3's residue: "I was left genuinely wanting to know what happens next - the right feeling for a mid-story stop."

**Why protect this one above all:** it is the only beat in the corpus that is *simultaneously* the best in all five categories, and it is on a route (Stray) that is *less* contaminated by the CONSATTENTION_DEATH chains. It is the proof that the story knows how to do this — the 031 sequence is the template the Antiquarian/Temple convergence scenes (022a, 022b, 040e) should be rewritten to match. The story's highest audience-experience peak is *not* in the canonical head; it is in the Stray route. That is a structural finding: the canonical head (040g) is emotionally the strongest (P'taxx's cost) but *experientially* the weakest (the "still" chains that flatten its own peaks), and the Stray route is *experientially* the strongest but has no comparable *emotional* cost. The story's two best routes are its two most different routes, and the convergence scenes that join them are its weakest. Protecting 031 means protecting the *pattern* (continuous escalation, zero restatement, causally-planted surprise, specific physical cost, real choice under threat) and rewriting the convergence scenes to match it.
