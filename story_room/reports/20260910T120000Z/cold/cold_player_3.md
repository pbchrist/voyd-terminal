# COLD PLAYER 3 — Stray Route Walk

Route walked (Stray): 000-the-fourth-bell → 030-the-cat-who-heard-it-first → 031-the-thing-below-the-courtyard → 040c-the-second-question (ACTIVE FRONTIER, no further choice links)

WALK_UNTIL: 040c-the-second-question.md

CHOICES_MADE:
- 000-the-fourth-bell -> "Hide the fragment and follow the stray" — (assigned Stray route) I wanted to go with the cat who already recognized me rather than hand the fragment to a bookseller or a temple, because the one who knew was the most dangerous and therefore the most interesting.
- 030-the-cat-who-heard-it-first -> "Open the cistern before the stray can stop you" — I wanted to see the impossible thing before it reached the top, to be the one who chose to look rather than to be the one who was kept from looking; I wanted the payoff of seeing what the stranger had hidden.

WANTED: As a player I wanted the story to make my decisions feel like they actually bent the world — that following the stray, and then heaving the lid up before he could stop me, produced consequences I had earned rather than consequences that would have happened anyway. I wanted the "measure/look-away" mechanic to feel like a real threat my choices could dodge, not a script the story was running me through.

FEARED: That the fragment's inner voice ("*Ask him what he threw in*", "*Look at him*", "*Look away*") and the recurring flinch motif would make me feel like I was being puppeted — that the story was deciding my character's impulses for me and only letting me "choose" which pre-written outcome to read, so that my agency was an illusion layered over a single linear plot.

ATTENTION_HIGH: 031-the-thing-below-the-courtyard, the moment the lid slid and there was no water but a night sky inside, and then the falling kit opened its orange eyes and said "You took your time." That was the single most gripping beat of the whole walk — the reveal that the stray did not find a fragment but escaped from one, and that the kit was what the Voyd learned to wear, all landed in one image.

ATTENTION_LOW: 030-the-cat-who-heard-it-first, the opening stretch of alley-following and back-and-forth dialogue ("You wrapped it?" / "No." / "Good." / "P'taxx likes things that can be shelved."). The setup was competent but slow, and the real pressure (the knocks from the cistern) didn't arrive until the scene's final third, so my engagement dipped during the middle cat-and-mouse conversation.

CONFUSED: In 031, the sentence "The Voyd. The name is not new. It is the door in the room behind the shop, seen from inside — the same ledger of looking-aways, the same filed fragment, the same countdown" arrived out of nowhere. On the Stray route I have never been inside the room behind the shop, never met the high door or the filed fragment, and the Antiquarian route is a different path I did not take — so the story is referencing shared canon/lore I have not been given, which briefly broke the "first-time player" illusion and made the world feel like it had a fixed back-story I was being dropped into rather than discovering.

REMAINED: The aftertaste is a clean, escalating dread: a thing that records your involuntary flinches and files them as evidence, a stranger who escaped one of these and carried his name under his skin, and a jammed cistern lid that can no longer close. The ending sits as a held breath — the kit named me, the lid is stuck open, and the frontier says the next move must confront the kit without letting the fragment choose my response first. It left me genuinely wanting to know what happens next, which is the right feeling for a mid-story stop.

CHOICE_INTEGRITY:
- 000-the-fourth-bell ("Hide the fragment and follow the stray" vs. "Take the fragment to P'taxx" vs. "Give the fragment to the temple"): REAL choice. Three genuinely different actions under pressure — hide it, hand it to a knowledgeable bookseller, or surrender it to a containment order — and each one opens a visibly different route (the Antiquarian route, the Temple route, the Stray route) with different companions, rooms, and stakes. Consequences actually diverge.
- 030-the-cat-who-heard-it-first ("Open the cistern before the stray can stop you" vs. "Force the stray to explain the scar"): REAL choice in form but WEAK in consequence. The two options are two different actions under real pressure (open the lid now vs. stall for the truth), so it feels like a real fork at the moment of choosing. But the payoff is suspicious: the "Force the stray to explain the scar" option's own tagline says "Whatever is climbing can wait three more breaths for the truth," and 032-twelve-years-ago opens with you literally counting "One. Two. Three." before you open the cistern — i.e., the truth-stall path funnels you back to the same open-the-cistern beat a few lines later, and both branches converge on 040c-the-second-question. So while the two actions are distinct in the moment, their downstream destinations are the same scene, which makes the choice feel like it's only buying a different flavor of the same next scene rather than two different worlds.

FIRST_FAILURE: 031-the-thing-below-the-courtyard, at the line "The Voyd. The name is not new. It is the door in the room behind the shop, seen from inside — the same ledger of looking-aways, the same filed fragment, the same countdown." This is the earliest moment the story stopped feeling like it was happening BY me and started happening TO me: the narration suddenly assumes I have interior knowledge (the room behind the shop, the filed fragment, the countdown) that the Stray route has never given me, which breaks the first-time-player contract and makes the world feel pre-written around a fixed back-story rather than unfolding from my choices. (The fragment's inner-voice impulses — "*Ask him what he threw in*", "*Look at him*" — are a close second; they nudge my character's decisions in a way that reads as the story pre-selecting my response rather than me freely choosing.)

VERDICT: The story respected my agency at the macro level (the three-way fork at the bell and the cistern fork both presented genuinely different actions under pressure and led to real, divergent routes), but it failed me at the moment the Stray route collided with shared canon — the "Voyd / room behind the shop / filed fragment" line in 031 dropped me into knowledge I had never earned, and the fragment's inner-voice impulses repeatedly pre-scripted my character's impulses, so that by the end my choices felt less like authorship and more like picking which pre-written beat to read.
