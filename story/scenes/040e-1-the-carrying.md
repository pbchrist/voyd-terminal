# The Carrying

You open the satchel.

Tomorrow's correction is still sealed, still riding against your spine, the tube's cold glass the only thing in the street that is not moving. The witnesses watch your paws the way they watched the dyer sign. They will carry whatever you do.

You take the tube out.

The street does not cheer. It does not flinch. It repeats. The chimney sweep's empty paw closes over the tube you do not yet hold, and the fish seller's rises to meet it, and the gesture is already a hundred paws deep before your own are on the glass. You are the one the record is about.

You walk.

The carrying is not a line. It is a net. Every cat on Mistral Street who watched you finish the shift now holds the shape of the handoff, and the net closes over the tube the way it closed over the dyer's signed name. You feel the pull of it in your paws: not weight, but attention. The method does not need to startle you. It has recruited the shape of your work, and the shape is walking with you, a hundred paws ahead, a hundred paws behind, each one a witness who can keep the carrying for the next.

The tube knocks against your ribs with every step. The sound travels down the net. The net carries it toward the palace.

You do not look at the palace. You do not have to. The city already knows how you will carry it, and the city is carrying it with you, and the carrying is the record, and the record is you.

The stray walks on your left. He does not speak. He carries the dead sliver in its blue cloth, and the dead sliver is the only thing in the street that is not repeating. He looks at the tube in your paws.

"Your record," he says. "The one that was never finished. It's still in your other paw."

You look down. It is. The unfinished record is still open under your paw, the one that was never filed, the one the method will turn back to when it finishes the Mistral's sheet. You are carrying two records now. One is the work. One is you.

The net carries them both.

The palace door is a hand's width ahead, and the net is already on the far side of it, a hundred paws deep, each one a witness who can keep the carrying for the next. The tube knocks against your ribs one more time.

You put it through.

The door closes behind you with the sound of the high door in the room you left. The net stays on the far side. The dead sliver in the stray's paw is the only thing on your side that does not repeat.

You are still carrying.

## ◉ ACTIVE FRONTIER

The method has the shape of your work. Your own unfinished record is the one it has not finished — it stays open under your paw, the one the method will turn back to when it finishes the Mistral's sheet. The next move must decide whether the keeping releases the subject it holds open, or whether the room re-closes the record the way the palace closes things.
