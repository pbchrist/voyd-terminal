# The Temple Door

The nearest Voreath acolyte sees the fragment in your paw and changes direction so sharply his claws spark against the stone.

“Do not close your paw.”

You had not realized you were doing exactly that.

The black object rests against your pads, colder than winter iron. Its orange seam pulses once.

*He will call it dangerous because he has no name for hungry.*

You nearly drop it.

The acolyte reaches you with the reliquary open. He is young, younger than the silver stitched into his robe suggests, and breathing too quickly.

“Put it inside.”

“What is it?”

“If I knew, I would not be carrying this.”

That answer earns him your trust by one whisker.

You lower the fragment toward the iron box.

The fourth bell rings.

The reliquary slams shut before the fragment touches it.

The acolyte stares at the box in his own paws.

“I did not—”

A second impact sounds beneath Faelspire.

This one is not a bell.

It is a door being struck from the other side.

The paving stones between you split in a perfect circle.

Every cat within ten paces recoils. The crack does not widen. No smoke rises from it. Instead, pale rootlike lines spread through the stone, branching outward in silence until they touch the acolyte's shadow.

He goes rigid.

His shadow moves without him.

It turns its head toward you.

“Run,” he says.

His mouth barely moves.

The shadow lifts one forepaw.

The acolyte does not.

Behind him, the other two Voreath cats stop dead. One whispers a prayer. The other draws a short ceremonial blade and immediately seems ashamed of how useless it looks.

The first acolyte forces the reliquary into your chest.

“Take both.”

“The box rejected it.”

“No.” His eyes remain fixed on his own shadow. “The box recognized it.”

The iron lid is open against your chest. You can feel the name waiting in it. Not a word. A shape. The shape of the one thing you have never let the city hold.

The shadow points toward the temple road.

Then toward the alley where the soot-gray stray vanished.

Two directions.

Two warnings.

Or two invitations.

The acolyte finally looks at you.

“Whatever you choose, do it before my shadow chooses for us.”

You know what the temple wants before the acolyte says it.

The reliquary is not a box. It is a name. The iron lid does not close around what is inside. It closes around what the lid says. And the lid has been waiting for a name. And the name it is waiting for is the one the city does not know. The one that has let you be a face the square forgets. The one that has let you be unmeasured.

The acolyte has seen you look.

“You know what it is,” he says.

“I know what it wants.”

“It wants to name you.”

The fragment in your paw pulses once.

*He is right.*

If you go to the temple, the lid will close. And the name under the name will be on the iron. And the iron does not give it back.
