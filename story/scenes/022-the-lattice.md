# The Lattice

The fourth bell rings beneath Faelspire.

Every window in the shop turns black.

Not dark.

Black.

Outside disappears.

The fragment on the table opens its orange seam.

Both seams widen.

A picture forms between them.

Not an image on the air. A depth.

Faelspire hangs beneath your paws as though the floor has become glass. Streets, towers, palace, temple, walls. Below all of it runs a branching lattice of pale lines.

Roots.

No.

Roads.

No.

Choices.

You know that without knowing how.

At the center of the lattice is a dark knot directly beneath Gistli Square.

Three orange points move toward it.

One is here.

One is somewhere under Aard Templu Voreath.

The third is moving quickly through the old drainage quarter.

“The soot-gray one,” you say. “The stray from the alley.”

The map changes.

The three orange points stop moving.

A fourth appears.

Not beneath Faelspire.

Above it.

High over the Royal Palace.

Something enormous casts a shadow across the city, though the three suns are still shining.

The fragment speaks into the room.

*The bell is not calling from below.*

The shop ceiling groans.

*It is measuring how long you take to look up.*

You look up.

Not because the fragment told you to. Because the measuring is almost finished, and the shadow above is almost done falling, and you want to see it before it is done.

It is not a flinch. It is not a refusal. It is a look-up.

Your paws leave the floor.

The lattice below you inverts. The city is a ceiling now, and the fourth point is a floor, and the shadow is a hand reaching down through both. The orange seam in your paw burns, and the fragment speaks — not into your head this time, but out of the space between you and the thing above.

*It measures looking away.*

*It has no word for looking back.*

But the record is already open.

A record it cannot see cannot complete. A witnessed flinch is the fuel. You gave it more than a flinch. You looked up. The look-up is the witness the measurement needed, and the witness completes the record, and the record, completed, closes on the name it has been measuring across all three routes.

The shadow above stops.

Not fades. Stops. The hand freezes halfway through its reach, and the thing it belongs to — vast, patient, shaped like the city's own accumulated silence — turns its attention downward. Toward you. Toward the one cat who looked up before the measurement was finished.

The shop ceiling groans again.

This time it does not groan.

It cracks.

The lattice holds the moment.

The fourth point hangs over the Royal Palace, the shadow hangs over the city, and the crack runs down the ceiling where the shop was, and the room learns what it carried in.

The record completes, and the one name the lattice has been measuring across all three routes spends itself three times — not as the lattice shows you the spends, but as the three routes register the look-up, each in its own causal history, each in its own body.

In P'taxx's shop, the high door registers it as the filing. The stone that has been waiting for the record takes it, and the ring the door has been making since the fragment slid into its light breaks in the middle of its note. The note snaps. It is a sound, and the sound is gone, and the filed name is in the door's stone, and the door is not going to give it back.

Under the Temple's iron, the reliquary registers it as the naming. The seam across the iron lid, which has been matching the seam in your paw since the square, closes the match. The two edges find each other with a click you feel through the table, through the stone, through your own paw. The named name is on the iron, and the iron is the lid, and the lid is open, and the name is the one the city does not know, and the iron does not give it back.

In the old drainage quarter, the stray's skin registers it as the replay. Under the torn ear, the scar that has run from the ear down the neck for twelve years splits. Not breaks. Splits — the way skin splits when a thing is coming through it. Pressure from beneath. The replayed name is surfacing under the fur for the first time, and the skin is under the scar, and the scar is under the ear, and the name is under the skin, and the skin is the one that is replaying.

The three registrations are not three different names.

They are the same name.

The one the city does not know.

The one that has let you be a face the square forgets.

The one that has let you be unmeasured.

You looked up.

That was the act.

The filing, the naming, the replay — those are what the act did. The door, the iron, the skin did not spend your anonymity. You spent it, with the look-up, before the measurement was finished. The record needed a witness, and you were it, and the record, completed, spent the name it had been measuring three times, in three bodies, in three irrevocabilities.

The cat who walked in is not the cat who walks out.

The cat who walks out is filed, and named, and replayed.

And the cat who walks out is the one the lattice holds — not because the lattice chose to hold it, but because the look-up completed the record, and the record holds what completes it, and you cannot un-look.
