# The Thing Below the Courtyard

You reach the cistern before the stray does.

The stone lid is heavier than it looks. You get both forepaws beneath the lip and heave.

“Don't.”

The word comes from behind you and from inside the cistern at the same time.

You pull anyway.

The lid slides aside.

Cold air rises out of the shaft.

There is no water below.

There is sky.

You are looking down into a night full of stars.

The problem is that Faelspire is still under daylight.

The three suns burn above the courtyard. Their light touches the rim of the cistern and stops. Inside, constellations you do not know turn slowly around a black center.

The stray grabs your shoulder.

“Close it.”

A shape crosses the stars.

Not flying.

Falling upward.

It grows larger.

Your orange fragment burns against your paw.

*Ask him what he threw in.*

You look at the stray.

His face answers before he does.

“Twelve years ago,” you say. “You put something down there.”

He lets go of you.

The thing inside the impossible sky is close enough now to have a silhouette.

A cat.

Small.

A kit.

The stray makes a sound so quiet you almost miss it.

“No.”

The falling kit opens its eyes.

Orange.

The same black line that marks the stray runs from one torn ear down its neck.

The kit smiles.

“You took your time,” it says.

The stray staggers backward.

You understand in a flash that feels like stepping onto missing stone.

The cat beside you did not find a fragment twelve years ago.

He escaped from one.

The kit in the cistern is what did not escape with him.

Or what he left behind.

Or what the Voyd learned to wear after he ran.

The fourth bell rings.

This time it comes from the open cistern.

The night sky fractures.

Through the cracks you glimpse something vast moving above Faelspire.

The stray looks from the broken sky to you.

“If that thing gets out,” he says, “it will remember being me.”

The kit reaches one paw toward the rim.

“And if you close it,” the kit says, “he never gets to remember being whole.”
