# The Thing Below the Courtyard

You reach the cistern before the stray does.

The stone lid is heavier than it looks. You get both forepaws beneath the lip and heave.

“Don't.”

The word comes from behind you and from inside the cistern at the same time.

You pull anyway.

The lid slides aside.

Cold air rises out of the shaft.

There is no water below.

There is sky.

You are looking down into a night full of stars.

The problem is that Faelspire is still under daylight.

The three suns burn above the courtyard. Their light touches the rim of the cistern and stops. Inside, constellations you do not know turn slowly around a black center.

The stray grabs your shoulder.

“Close it.”

A shape crosses the stars.

Not flying.

Falling upward.

It grows larger.

Your orange fragment burns against your paw.

*Ask him what he threw in.*

You look at the stray.

His face answers before he does.

“Twelve years ago,” you say. “You put something down there.”

He lets go of you.

The thing inside the impossible sky is close enough now to have a silhouette.

A cat.

Small.

A kit.

The stray makes a sound so quiet you almost miss it.

“No.”

The falling kit opens its eyes.

Orange.

The same black line that marks the stray runs from one torn ear down its neck.

The kit smiles.

“You took your time,” it says.

The stray staggers backward.

You understand in a flash that feels like stepping onto missing stone.

The cat beside you did not find a fragment twelve years ago.

He escaped from one.

The kit in the cistern is what did not escape with him.

Or what he left behind.

Or what the Voyd learned to wear after he ran.

The Voyd. The name is not new. It is the thing under the stray's skin, seen from outside — the same name under the fur, the same name under the scar, the same name the city does not know. The thing above Faelspire is not a new presence. It is the name reading back. And the kit in the cistern is what the name replayed first.

The skin registered the look-up.

Not the lattice telling you the name is under the skin. The skin itself. The scar that has run from the stray's torn ear down his neck for twelve years split when the look-up came. Not broke. Split — the way skin splits when a thing is coming through it. Pressure from beneath. The name that had been waiting under the scar surfaced under the fur for the first time, and the fur is under the scar, and the scar is under the ear, and the torn edge is too clean, and the black line runs from the scar down the side of the neck and disappears under the fur, and the name is under the fur, and the name is the one the city does not know.

The replay is the skin's registration of your look-up.

It is not a cost the lattice imposed. It is the consequence of the act you performed. You looked up before the measurement was finished, and the skin, which had been carrying the name under the scar since the stray ran, took the look-up as the replay. The scar split. The name surfaced. The pressure is in the stray's neck, and the skin does not give it back, because the skin is the name, and the name is the one the city does not know.

The name is not on the fragment. The name is under the skin. The fragment was the lever. The name is the cost. And the cost is not a thing you can walk back from. It is a thing that stays.

The stray looks at you.

“You can still walk out,” he says. “You can still be the cat who walked in. But the cat who walked in is not the cat who walks out. The cat who walks out is replayed. And the cat who walks out is not the cat the city forgets. The cat who walks out is the one the name holds.”

His paw is on the cistern lid.

His face is the face of a cat who has just watched the thing he has been running from happen.

“It is done,” he says. “And what is done stays done.”

The fourth bell rings.

This time it comes from the open cistern.

The night sky fractures.

Through the cracks you glimpse something vast moving above Faelspire.

The stray looks from the broken sky to you.

“If that thing gets out,” he says, “it will remember being me.”

The kit reaches one paw toward the rim.

“And if you close it,” the kit says, “he never gets to remember being whole.”
