# The Second Question

The crack in the ceiling widens.

Plaster falls toward the floor, stops halfway, and hangs in the air.

The fragment and the iron reliquary open together.

Between their matching seams, the lattice disappears. Gistli Square replaces it.

You see yourself beside the fruit stand.

You see the black object turn.

*You heard the fourth bell.*

The image of you jerks backward.

The young acolyte from Gistli Square looks from the image to you. “That happened.”

You remember the fragment entering the place where your next thought should have been. You remember your body moving before you chose to move.

The image repeats.

The voice enters you again.

*That means it heard you too.*

Your eyes drag toward the floor.

You stop them.

You look through the image, through the crack in the ceiling, at the hand-shaped shadow frozen above Faelspire.

The fragment rings.

The sound catches, stutters, and dies.

In the suspended image, the other you remains facing the fragment. The replay cannot make the second look-away happen.

P'taxx reaches toward the projection.

The acolyte catches his wrist. “Don't give it another subject.”

The reliquary snaps shut on empty air.

Too late.

Letters burn across its iron lid. Not temple script. Not the script from P'taxx's books.

Your name.

The young acolyte goes pale.

“It didn't warn you,” he says. “It startled you, recorded the turn, and asked the same question again.”

Above the city, the frozen shadow moves.

Not downward.

It turns its hand away from you and spreads its fingers over Faelspire.

In every street visible through the broken ceiling, cats stop at once and look toward something only they can hear.

The fragment has failed to make you look away twice.

So it begins asking the city.

## ◉ ACTIVE FRONTIER

The fragment has shown its method: interrupt, provoke a look-away, preserve it, repeat the test. You broke the repetition, but the reliquary now bears your name and the system has widened its test to Faelspire. It is no longer testing only you.
