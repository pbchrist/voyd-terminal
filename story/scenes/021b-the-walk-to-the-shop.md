# The Walk to the Shop

The young acolyte walks you across Gistli Square.

The one who pressed the iron box against your chest.

The fragment is bare in your paw, and its orange seam has not stopped pulsing since the square split open beneath it.

The iron reliquary is open in the other acolyte's paws. It has been open since the square. You have been walking with the name waiting in the lid the whole time. Not a word. A shape. The shape of the one thing you have never let the city hold.

Behind you, the other two acolytes keep their distance. The square still smells of overturned fruit stalls and fear.

The walk is shorter than it should be.

The shop door is unlocked when they reach it.

You open it.

P'taxx is already inside, standing where a shopkeeper should not stand: behind the table, not in front of it, facing the door.

He looks at the box in the acolyte's paws.

Then at you.

“You brought it to me.”

“You sent him to me.”

The acolyte does not answer.

The fragment's seam widens in your paw, as though it has heard something you have not.

“You sent him to me,” you repeat. “And now it's measuring. The thing above — it started when the fourth bell rang. It's counting down. And I have to walk through this square carrying the only thing it wants to read.”

The acolyte stops.

“Counting down to what?”

“To me looking up.”

The acolyte's silence is different now. Not evasive. Calculating. The acolyte has heard the timer before, in a different form — the temple's own bells, the ones that mark the interval between the third and fourth ring. The acolyte knows what happens at the end of that interval.

“Then we walk faster,” the acolyte says.

But the fragment in your paw pulses once, slowly, and the orange seam narrows.

*It does not care how fast you walk. It cares how long you take to look up.*

The acolyte looks at the seam. Looks at you. Looks at the sky above the square, where something vast is already casting a shadow that the three suns cannot account for.

“Then,” the acolyte says, “I hope you are a slow looker.”

The acolyte sets the iron reliquary on the table.

The lid is open.

The name is on the iron.

Not burned. Not written. There. The way a name is there when you have been named and the naming has not finished. The iron lid does not hold the name. The iron lid is the name. And the lid is open. And the name is on the lid. And the lid is on the table. And the table is in P'taxx's shop.

P'taxx does not look at you.

He looks at the lid.

And he looks at the name on the lid.

And he does not say what the name is. He does not need to. You do not know what the name is. But you know it is yours. You know it is the one the city does not know. You know it is the one that has let you be a face the square forgets. You know it is the one that has let you be unmeasured. And you know it is on the iron now. And the iron does not give it back.

“You named me,” you say.

P'taxx does not answer.

The young acolyte answers.

“The temple does not name,” he says. “The temple contains. The name is the containment. The name is what the temple holds. And the name is yours. And the name is on the iron.”

P'taxx looks at the acolyte.

“And the name?” he says.

The acolyte does not answer.

The name is on the iron.

The name is not in the iron. The name is the iron. And the iron is the lid. And the lid is open. And the lid is on the table. And the table is in the shop. And the shop is not the temple. And the temple is not the shop. And the name is on the iron.

The name is filed in the iron.

The name is not on the fragment. The name is on the iron. The fragment was the lever. The name is the cost. And the cost is not a thing you can walk back from. It is a thing that stays.

The young acolyte looks at you.

“You can still walk out,” he says. “You can still be the cat who walked in. But the cat who walked in is not the cat who walks out. The cat who walks out is named. And the cat who walks out is not the cat the city forgets. The cat who walks out is the one the temple holds.”

P'taxx's paw is on your shoulder.

His face is the face of a cat who has just watched the thing he has been waiting for happen.

“It is done,” he says. “And what is done stays done.”
