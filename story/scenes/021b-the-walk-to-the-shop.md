# The Walk to the Shop

The young acolyte walks you across Gistli Square.

The one who pressed the iron box against your chest.

The fragment is bare in your paw, its orange seam still pulsing since the square split open. The iron reliquary is open in the other acolyte's paws, and you have been walking with the name waiting in the lid the whole time. Not a word. A shape. The shape of the one thing you have never let the city hold.

Behind you, the other two acolytes keep their distance. The square still smells of fear.

The walk is shorter than it should be. The shop door is unlocked when they reach it.

P'taxx is already inside, behind the table, facing the door.

He looks at the box in the acolyte's paws.

Then at you.

“You brought it to me.”

“You sent him to me.”

The acolyte does not answer.

"You sent him to me," you repeat. "And now it's measuring. It started when the fourth bell rang. It's counting down. And I have to carry the only thing it wants to read through this square."

The acolyte stops.

"Counting down to what?"

"To me looking up."

The acolyte's silence is different now. Not evasive. Calculating. He has heard the timer before, in the temple's bells. He knows what happens at the end of that interval.

“Then we walk faster,” the acolyte says.

But the fragment pulses once, slowly, and the orange seam narrows.

*It does not care how fast you walk. It cares how long you take to look up.*

The acolyte looks at the seam. Looks at the sky above the square, where something vast is already casting a shadow the suns cannot account for.

“Then,” the acolyte says, “I hope you are a slow looker.”

The acolyte sets the iron reliquary on the table.

The name is on the iron.

Not burned. Not written. In motion. The iron lid is the transit, and the transit is walking.

P'taxx does not look at you.

He looks at the lid.

And he does not say what the name is. He does not need to. You do not know what the name is. But you know it is yours. You know it is the one the city does not know. And it is walking with you now.

"You named me," you say.

P'taxx does not answer.

The young acolyte answers.

“The temple does not name,” he says. "The temple contains. The name is what the temple holds. And the name is yours. And the name is in the iron."

P'taxx looks at the acolyte.

“And the name?” he says.

The acolyte does not answer.

The name is in the iron.

It is not filed here. It is in transit. The temple brought the box to hold the name, and the walk is finishing that containing.

The young acolyte looks at you.

"The cat who walks out is named," he says. "And the cat who walks out is not the cat the city forgets. The cat who walks out is the one the temple holds.”

P'taxx's paw is on your shoulder.

“It is done,” he says. “And what is done stays done.”
