# The Temple Takes the Test

You set the fragment on the reliquary's lid.

The iron is already open. The seam in your paw matches the seam in the iron the way a key matches a lock it was cut for. The acolyte does not reach for the fragment. He waits, because the temple's method is to wait, and the fragment has been waiting longer than the temple.

The fragment turns on the lid.

Not toward you. Toward the acolyte. Its seam opens, and the voice that entered your thought in Gistli Square enters his instead.

*You heard the fourth bell.*

The acolyte's grip on the lid does not shake this time. He heard it in Gistli Square, in the crowd, before the acolytes pushed through. He heard it the way the city heard it, and he has been carrying the hearing ever since.

"I know," he says.

The fragment's seam closes. The lid closes over it with the sound of a seal being signed. The letters on the iron — your name — settle into the metal the way a filing settles into a drawer.

P'taxx looks at the closed iron. "The box holds it. The box does not unname what it read."

"No," the acolyte says. "The box holds both."

He looks at you. "The test stops when the iron holds the fragment. The iron holds the fragment when someone carries the iron when it wakes. The iron bears your name. You are the one it will send for."

You look at the satchel on your back. Two years of sealed corrections, the palace's seals, the shops' seals, the temple's seals. The satchel has been the one thing in Faelspire that every seal trusts. The acolyte is asking it to carry a seal it was never meant to carry.

"The palace signs its corrections out," you say.

"Yes," the acolyte says. "The temple will sign you in."

The fishmonger's knife is still halfway through the silverbelly across the square. The courier pigeon is still wheeled back over the roof. The city is still, and the stillness is the test waiting for the iron to say otherwise.

You take the iron.

It is heavier than the fragment was. It is heavier than the satchel. It bears a name that is not yours to carry.

The acolyte steps back from the table. He does not follow you to the door. He watches you leave the way the temple watches a containment: without hope, without fear, with the certainty that the iron will wake and that the one it sends for will come.

P'taxx blocks the doorway one more time. "You are carrying the temple's method out of my shop."

"I am carrying the test," you say.

"The test is the one it will ask again," he says. "The iron is the one that will hold the asking. You are the one it will send. Those are three different things, and you are carrying all three."

You leave the shop with the iron on your back and the satchel on your front. The city is still. The test is waiting. The iron bears your name, and the name is not yours to spend.

## ◉ ACTIVE FRONTIER

The fragment is contained in iron that bears the protagonist's name. The test is stopped, and the stopping is a debt: the temple will send for the protagonist when the iron wakes, and the acolyte's trust is now a ledger the temple keeps. The satchel's neutrality is spent — the palace-facing work now carries a temple mark, and the palace, which signs its corrections out, will notice a seal it did not sign. The next move must address what the iron does when it wakes, whether the protagonist can refuse to be sent, and what the palace does when its runner's satchel carries a seal the palace never issued.
