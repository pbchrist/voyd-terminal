# The Runner Keeps the Fragment

You close the satchel over the fragment.

The acolyte's paw is still on the reliquary's lid. You do not take the iron. You do not explain. The temple's method is to contain, and the containment is the offer, and the offer is the one the temple makes when it has no other move.

"Temple contains," the acolyte says.

"Temple does not chase," you say.

He looks at you the way the temple looks at a danger it cannot hold: without fear, without hope, with the certainty that the thing will be asked again. He does not step between you and the door. The two temple cats behind him do not move. The blade stays sheathed.

P'taxx unblocks the doorway. "You are carrying the test out of my shop."

"I am carrying the work," you say.

"The work is the one the palace signs out," he says. "The test is the one the city signs in. Those are two different things, and you are carrying both."

You leave the shop with the fragment in your satchel and the satchel on your back. The acolyte does not follow. The city is still, and the stillness is the test waiting to be asked.

The fragment turns in the satchel against your spine. Its seam opens, and the voice that entered your thought in Gistli Square enters the street instead.

*You heard the fourth bell.*

Not you. The fishmonger with the knife halfway through the silverbelly. The courier pigeon wheeled back over the roof. The mother with two kits beneath the cart. The question is not in your head anymore. The question is in the street.

The satchel. The seals. The weight of the unfinished. The city measures a runner by the satchel, and the satchel carries the method now.

Every correction you deliver is a question asked of a cat who must sign. The dyer on Mistral Street will sign for the error in the name that made it. The palace files the dyer under her dead mother's name, and the correction is the one that fixes it, and the fixing is the one the method asks the city to witness.

The fragment's seam closes. The satchel settles against your spine. The street is still, and the stillness is the test waiting for the next delivery.

The acolyte's face is the last thing you see through the shop window. He is watching the street. He will remember the refusal.
