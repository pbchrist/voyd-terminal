# The Cat Who Heard It First

The stray does not look back until Gistli Square is three alleys behind you.

When he finally does, it is only to make sure you still have the fragment.

“You wrapped it?” he asks.

“No.”

“Good.”

“That is the opposite of what P'taxx said.”

“P'taxx likes things that can be shelved.”

He keeps moving.

Up close you see that the missing half of his ear was not torn away. The edge is too clean. A thin black line runs from the scar down the side of his neck and disappears under his fur.

The fourth bell sounds again.

He winces before it rings.

“You knew.”

“I knew when it was going to happen.”

“How?”

“Because mine rings first.”

He slips through a broken gate into a courtyard abandoned to nettles and rain barrels. You follow.

Only when the gate swings shut does he stop.

“Show me.”

You open your paw.

The fragment's orange seam brightens.

The black line under the stray's fur answers.

Light moves beneath his skin.

You take one involuntary step backward.

He laughs once, without humor.

“Now you understand why I did not stay in the square.”

“What happened to you?”

“I picked one up twelve years ago.”

The courtyard seems to contract around that sentence.

“You've had one of these for twelve years?”

“No.”

He touches the scar below his ear.

“It had me for three minutes.”

The orange seam in your fragment opens.

*He is lying.*

The stray's pupils narrow.

“You heard that.”

You say nothing.

“That is worse than I thought.”

He moves to an old rain cistern and kicks loose a brick near its base. From the hollow behind it he removes a strip of blue cloth wrapped around something long and narrow.

He unfolds it.

A sliver of the same black material lies inside.

Dead.

No orange light.

No voice.

Your fragment turns toward it.

The dead sliver cracks.

The stray lunges for the cloth.

Too late.

A sound comes from inside the cistern.

Three soft knocks.

The stray goes white around the muzzle.

“No,” he whispers.

The knocks come again.

Then a voice rises from the empty stone chamber.

Your voice.

“Do not let him open it.”

You stare at the stray.

He stares at you.

Then the cistern answers in his voice.

“Do not let *them* close it.”

Something begins climbing the inside wall.
