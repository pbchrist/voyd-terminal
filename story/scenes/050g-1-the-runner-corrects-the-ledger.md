# The Runner Corrects the Ledger

The satchel is at your side. It has been since the shop.

You know which scroll is inside before you touch it. You have carried the city's corrections for two years, and you never opened a seal twice, but the ones the palace should not have are marked by a fold. The Mistral's is the newest. A dyer's daughter, filed in the palace's first season under a name that was never hers. The seal is unbroken. It is still open to you.

You learned it just now, the one new rule: the method works the edge it has, and the edge is under your paw, and the paw does not come off. The aim is gone. What is left is the working, and the working takes whatever sheet lies at the edge.

You use the rule before the rule can be used on you.

You lift your other paw. You take the Mistral's scroll from the satchel. You lay it under the edge of the sheet, and you pull.

The method does not ask whose paw. It works the edge. The seal breaks in the pale light, and the scroll unrolls across the black the way the square once unrolled: the name as the palace filed it, the name as the cat lived, the fold where the error was. The Mistral's record moves across the room like the square did, and where it settles it is not filed — it is corrected. Under the name the palace should not have used, a new line: the name the cat actually went by, held legible, held where the keeping holds it.

Your own record is still open under the other paw. It has not moved. The method will turn back to it when it is done. You have bought time the only way the room will sell it: with a name it should not have.

The satchel is lighter. The seal is broken, and you will never close it. The palace signs its corrections out. It will notice a seal it did not sign, and it will know which runner's paws were on it.

The door is still open. P'taxx's face is still on the cold stone, and the keeping still holds him open around the desk — the subject is legible, not buried. The method is working the Mistral's record, and you are the hand that gave it.
