# Voreath Enters the Shop

The temple cats arrive at Antiquarian Books & Sundries with the iron reliquary already open.

P'taxx blocks the doorway.

“No.”

The young acolyte beside you gives him a look that would embarrass a palace guard.

“You have not heard what we are asking.”

“You brought Voreath to my shop carrying a box designed to recognize things you do not understand. I have heard enough.”

You stepped out of this shop with a wrapped fragment under one paw and an old bookseller's quiet terror under the other.

The door was locked.

You unlocked it.

The two other acolytes are behind the young one, and they do not look as though they were in the square when the bell first rang.

The iron reliquary is open on the table between you and P'taxx.

It has been open since the square. The acolyte pressed it against your chest and said *take both*, and you have been carrying it across the square with your paw against the iron, and you have felt the name waiting in the lid the whole time. Not a word. A shape. The shape of the one thing you have never let the city hold.

Now the lid is open on the table, and the name is not in the lid anymore.

The name is on the iron.

Not burned. Not written. There. The way a name is there when you have been named and the naming has not finished. The iron lid does not hold the name. The iron lid is the name. And the lid is open. And the name is on the lid. And the lid is on the table. And the table is in P'taxx's shop.

P'taxx does not look at you.

He looks at the lid.

And he looks at the name on the lid.

And he does not say what the name is. He does not need to. You do not know what the name is. But you know it is yours. You know it is the one the city does not know. You know it is the one that has let you be a face the square forgets. You know it is the one that has let you be unmeasured. And you know it is on the iron now. And the iron does not give it back.

“You named me,” you say.

P'taxx does not answer.

The young acolyte answers.

“The temple does not name,” he says. “The temple contains. The name is the containment. The name is what the temple holds. And the name is yours. And the name is on the iron.”

P'taxx looks at the acolyte.

“And the name?” he says.

The acolyte does not answer.

The name is on the iron.

The name is not in the iron. The name is the iron. And the iron is the lid. And the lid is open. And the lid is on the table. And the table is in the shop. And the shop is not the temple. And the temple is not the shop. And the name is on the iron.

The name is filed in the iron.

The name is not on the fragment. The name is on the iron. The fragment was the lever. The name is the cost. And the cost is not a thing you can walk back from. It is a thing that stays.

The young acolyte looks at you.

“You can still walk out,” he says. “You can still be the cat who walked in. But the cat who walked in is not the cat who walks out. The cat who walks out is named. And the cat who walks out is not the cat the city forgets. The cat who walks out is the one the temple holds.”

P'taxx's paw is on your shoulder.

His face is the face of a cat who has just watched the thing he has been waiting for happen.

“It is done,” he says. “And what is done stays done.”
