# Voreath Enters the Shop

The temple cats arrive at Antiquarian Books & Sundries with the iron reliquary already open.

P'taxx blocks the doorway.

“No.”

The young acolyte beside you gives him a look that would embarrass a palace guard.

“You have not heard what we are asking.”

“You brought Voreath to my shop carrying a box designed to recognize things you do not understand. I have heard enough.”

The two other acolytes are behind the young one, and they do not look as though they were in the square when the bell first rang.

The iron reliquary is open on the table between you and P'taxx. It has been open since the square. The acolyte pressed it against your chest and said *take both*, and you have been carrying it across the square with your paw against the iron, and you have felt the name waiting in the lid the whole time. Not a word. A shape. The shape of the one thing you have never let the city hold.

Now the lid is open, and the name is on the iron.

Not burned. Not written. Held. The open lid has caught the shape you carried across the square and brought it into P'taxx's shop.

P'taxx does not look at you.

He looks at the lid.

He looks at the shape on the lid.

And he does not say what the name is. He does not need to. You do not know what the name is. But you know it is yours. You know it is the one the city does not know, the one that has let you be unmeasured. And you know it is in the iron now. And the iron does not give it back.

“You named me,” you say.

P'taxx does not answer.

The young acolyte answers.

“The temple does not name,” he says. “It contains. The iron is holding what belongs to you.”

P'taxx looks at the acolyte.

“And the name?” he says.

The acolyte does not answer.

The name is in the iron.

The containment is done in the shop, not at the lattice. The temple brought the box to hold the name, and the containing is a thing the temple did, not a thing the lattice will do. The name is not filed here. It is held. And the holding is yours to carry out of the shop.

The young acolyte looks at you.

“You can still walk out,” he says. “You can still be the cat who walked in. But the cat who walked in is not the cat who walks out. The cat who walks out is named. And the cat who walks out is not the cat the city forgets. The cat who walks out is the one the temple holds.”

P'taxx's paw is on your shoulder.

His face is the face of a cat who has just watched the thing he has been waiting for happen.

“It is done,” he says. “And what is done stays done.”
