# The Correction Through the Door

The satchel is over your shoulder. The door is the width of your shoulder.

You can go through it. You have carried things through doors for two years that were not yours and could not come back: sealed scrolls, names the palace should not have, the fold that marked the ones that were wrong. The satchel is the only thing in this room that still moves the way it always has.

Inside it is the Mistral's scroll. The dyer's daughter, filed under a name that was never hers, corrected in the palace's hand and never signed out. The seal is unbroken. It is still open to you, and you know what a runner does with a correction that is still open: the runner carries it to the door that fixes it.

You go through the door.

The lines do not follow you out. They stay behind, holding the record about you open in the room — open under the paws that are no longer there — until the method says otherwise. You have left your record where it can be read.

The keeping does not finish what it holds, but the method outranks the keeping, and the method has a new sheet now: the scroll in your paw. The door's edge broke the seal as you passed, and the Mistral's name is unrolling in the pale light on the far side of the seam. The room works it the way it worked the recall — the name as the palace filed it, the name as the cat lived, the fold where the error was. The room takes the correction. The Mistral's record is in the room now, and it is not filed. It is corrected.

The satchel is lighter. The seal is broken, and you will never close it.

The palace signs its corrections out. It will notice a seal it did not sign, and it will know which runner's paws were on it, because the runner never opened a seal twice — and this one is open.

Behind you, the door is the width of your shoulder. It will not be wider again. Your record is in the room, held open, and P'taxx is at the desk in the room, and the room keeps.
