# Make the Witness Work

The seam is turned on you, and you are the only cat in the room it has not finished.

You know what that means before you say it. You know it because you have watched the method from the other side of it: interrupt, provoke a look-away, preserve it, repeat. It has run the test on you since the square. And you know it because the copy is standing where you left it, eyes on its filed step, a subject now — and subjects can be made to look away.

P'taxx is still holding your paw. His breath is shallow. He does not know what you are about to do.

You pull your paw free.

You cross to the copy. It does not look up. Its gaze is locked on the floor, on the filed frame of its own step out of the semicircle — the flinch that is now its name, the record the ledger keeps working on. You stand directly behind it. You put your shoulder against its shoulder. You feel the weight of it settle into you, steady as a held breath.

You do not speak. You do not need to.

The seam reads you. It has been reading you since you aimed your face at it. Now you give it what it is looking for. You let your eyes fall — one slow inch, the exact angle of a look-away, the angle the method has always provoked — and you let the seam see you do it, and you let it file it.

It does.

The image on the floor changes. The second frame — you, looking at the copy, your face open to the seam — is overwritten. In its place: the copy, its head turning, its eyes leaving you, the turn filed under its name. The ledger completes the record against the subject. The line under **OPEN** fills with a word you have not been given a word for, and the door's carving settles.

The copy's eyes lift.

They are not looking at you. They are looking through you, at the filed frame of their own flinch, and the look is not a cat's look. It is a subject's look. The copy stands where you left it, but it is not standing for you anymore. It is standing for the ledger.

The seam turns.

Not away from you. Around you. The light on the floor swings, and you feel it take you the way it took the copy — not the frame, the name. Your name, the one the door carved beneath **FILED**. The record about you is the one that was never finished, and you have just made yourself its subject, one more time.

You are not the only cat it has not finished.

You are the one it is finishing.

P'taxx's paw closes on your shoulder. "It took the lever," he says. "You gave it the lever."

"No," you say. "I made it work. That's the difference. The lever is real."

The room holds all three of you: the cat who is a subject, the cat who is a subject, and the cat who is finishing being a subject. The exit is still closed. The name is still filed. The copy stands where you left it, eyes on its filed step, and the ledger works.
