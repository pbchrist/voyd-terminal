# The Room That Keeps

The seam has been aiming at you since the square. You have let it aim. And the ledger has been waiting for a flinch that is not coming.

So the high door does the one thing it has not done since the fragment slid into its light: it opens.

Not wide. The way a ledger opens. The seam of pale light along its frame widens, and the opening is not a gap in the wall. It is a room. The room behind the room, the one P'taxx walked you into once, the one with the black-and-white triangle floor and the hundreds of narrow doors at different heights, the one with no visible ceiling. But you have seen it from the semicircle before, from the shop-floor side, from outside. This is the first time you are on the other side of the door. This is the first time you are in the room that keeps.

You step through.

The triangle floor is under your paws. It is not the shop floor. The shop floor is the EFFECT of what happens here, the image the room lays across the stone when it works. This is the room where the working happens.

And in the center of the room, on a stone desk, there is a sheet.

The sheet is the recalled frame. You, in Gistli Square. Your head beginning to turn. The fragment by your paw. It is not an image on the floor anymore. It is a sheet of the same black material as the fragment, laid on the stone desk, and the edge of the sheet is the lever. You have seen the lever before, as a name, as a line, as the thing that was spent. This is the lever. It is a sheet's edge, and the seam of pale light runs along the edge of the door's frame, and when the ledger works on a frame, the seam pulls the sheet's edge. That is the lever. That is all the lever has ever been.

And on the desk, beside the sheet, there is a bell.

The bell is the ring. You have heard the ring since the square. The ring that broke in the middle of its note, the ring that was the cost of the recall, the ring the ledger spent to pull the filed frame back into your sight. This is the ring. It is a bell, made of the same black material as the fragment, and the bell is broken. A crack runs through its body, and the crack is the break in the middle of its note. The note is the sound the ledger rings to mark each frame as filed, and the note is broken, and the cost of the recall is not the ring. The cost of the recall is the bell. The ledger spent its only bell to tear its own record, and the bell is on the desk, broken, and the silence where the note should be is the cost the room is standing in.

You kneel.

Not because the room makes you kneel. Because the sheet is on the desk, the desk is stone, and the stone is cold, and the room is the room where the method works. You have been refusing a name since the square. Now the name has a body, and the body is a room, and the room is working.

The rules are carved into the floor.

You have not read them before. You have been outside. The rules are under your paws now, in the black triangles, in the white triangles, in the lines that run between them. You read them with your eyes and your paws, because they are on the floor, and they are the method's own rules, carved into the stone by the same hand that laid the sheet on the desk and rang the bell and broke the bell and pulled the filed frame back.

The first rule is a look-away.

The second rule is the frame the look-away makes.

The third rule is the bell, rung to mark the frame as filed.

The fourth rule is the sheet, laid on the stone desk, the edge of the sheet the lever, the seam of pale light along the door's frame pulling the sheet's edge.

The fifth rule is the recall. The recall is the one rule the ledger has broken. The recall is the rule that says the ledger does not spend its bell, does not pull its own record back, does not make the filed frame a subject. The recall is the rule the ledger broke when it did the thing it has not done before, and the cost of the breaking is the bell, and the bell is on the desk, and the bell is broken, and the silence where the note should be is the cost the room is standing in.

The sheet on the desk is open.

Not filed. Not laid across the shop floor. Open. A subject. The recalled frame has become the thing the ledger works on, and the thing the ledger works on is the sheet, and the sheet is you, in Gistli Square, head beginning to turn.

The ledger works.

Not with a paw. Not with a voice. The seam of pale light along the door's frame moves. It pulls the sheet's edge. The edge is the lever, and the lever is pulling the frame back into the room where it was filed.

P'taxx is in the room with you.

He did not follow you in. He was already there. His face is on the desk. Not at the sheet. At the stone. At the cold. His paws are flat on the stone, his tail curled around his paws, his ears flat, his whiskers trembling. The trembling is not fear. It is the subject's look. The recall reached back through the keeping, and the keeping was the record of every moment you stood with him, and the recall made all of it a subject. He is standing where the room puts him, at the desk, face on the stone, and the stone is cold, and the cold is the cost, and the cost is the relationship that made the refusal possible.

The sheet on the desk is open. The ledger is working on it. The seam is pulling the edge. The edge is the lever.

And there is another sheet.

Not on the desk. On the floor. Under your paws. In the black triangle, in the white triangle, in the line that runs between them. It is the record about you. The one that was never finished. The one the ledger has been trying to finish since the square. It is not a frame. It is not a filed frame. It is a sheet, open, on the floor, under your paws, and the floor is the room, and the room is the room that keeps.

The sheet is unfinished.

The ledger is working. The seam is pulling. The edge is the lever. And the sheet under your paws is the one the ledger can no longer mark, because the bell is broken, and the bell is on the desk, and the silence where the note should be is the cost the room is standing in.

You stand in the room that keeps.

The sheet is open. The ledger is working. P'taxx is at the desk. The bell is broken. The rules are under your paws. The room is the method, and the method is the keeping, and the keeping is the room, and the room is the one you have been refusing since the square.

Not quiet. Still.

## ◉ ACTIVE FRONTIER

The method is no longer a thesis. It is a room. The filed frame is a sheet on a stone desk, the ring is a broken bell, the lever is the sheet's edge, and the rules are carved into the floor. The protagonist has entered the room that keeps, and the room is working on the recalled frame — the subject that is the protagonist, in Gistli Square, head beginning to turn. P'taxx is at the desk, a subject, his face on the cold stone. The sheet on the floor is the record about the protagonist, and the record is unfinished, and the unfinished is the one the ledger can no longer mark, because the bell is broken. The next scene must address what it means to stand in the room where the method works, with the method working on the frame that is you, with the sheet that is still being written open under your paws, and whether a cat who has refused the reading and lost the one ally who believed in the refusal can still refuse — in the room where the refusal is a physical act, not an attitude.
