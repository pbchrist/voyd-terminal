# The Second Question

The kit reaches for the rim.

Your fragment speaks before its paw touches stone.

*Look at him.*

You look at the stray.

The kit jerks backward.

It is a small movement. Shoulders first. Chin lifting. Eyes leaving you.

Your movement.

The exact flinch from Gistli Square.

The stray sees recognition cross your face.

“What?”

“It moved like me.”

The kit smiles.

Then it does it again: the fragment turning on the stones, the voice entering your thought, your body retreating before you chose.

The impossible sky around it becomes the square for one heartbeat. Fruit rolls across stars. Cats flee between constellations.

The fragment burns in your paw.

*Look away.*

This time you understand the pressure before your body obeys it.

You keep your eyes on the kit.

The orange seam widens.

The kit flinches—but you do not.

Its smile slips. For an instant it wears your face, caught halfway between the movement the fragment recorded and the movement you refused to repeat.

The stray grips the cistern lid.

“What did you do?”

“Nothing.”

“No.” The kit's voice comes from the shaft and from inside your fragment. “You did not do what was written.”

Lines of orange light climb the cistern wall. They form the outline of your first recoil, then stop before the head can turn.

Beneath the unfinished outline, letters appear.

Your name.

The stray reads it aloud.

The kit's paw closes around the rim.

The stone splits under its claws. The lid drops crookedly into the crack and jams. It can no longer close the cistern.

Now you know what the fragment kept.

Not your fear.

The moment your fear made the choice for you.

## ◉ ACTIVE FRONTIER

The fragment replayed your first involuntary look-away through the kit, then tried to produce a second. You held your gaze, leaving the new record unfinished, but the cistern has named you and its lid can no longer close. The next move must confront the kit without letting the fragment choose your response first.
