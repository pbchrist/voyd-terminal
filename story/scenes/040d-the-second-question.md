# The Second Question

The dead sliver rings in the stray's paw.

The sound is thin and broken, but the black fragment answers it.

Orange light spills across the courtyard stones.

Gistli Square appears beneath your feet.

Not as it is now. As it was when the fourth bell rang.

The fruit stand. The fleeing crowd. The impossible object turning toward you.

A second you stands inside the light.

*You heard the fourth bell.*

The other you jerks backward.

You feel the movement in your own shoulders.

The stray closes his paw around the sliver. The image remains.

“It did that to you,” he says.

The fragment speaks into your next thought.

*That means it heard you too.*

The pressure comes with the words: turn toward the cistern, check the rooftops, look anywhere except at the image.

You keep watching.

The other you begins to recoil again.

This time it stops.

The projection shudders between two moments—one in which you look away, one in which you do not. The dead sliver screams like metal dragged across glass.

Under the stray's skin, your name lengthens by one letter.

Then stops.

He tears his paw open.

The sliver falls, still burning.

“Three minutes,” he says. “It kept asking until I moved the way it wanted. When I stopped answering, mine died.”

The fragment in your paw rings again.

The sliver on the stones does not answer.

Now you can see the difference. Your fragment holds a complete movement: the recoil it forced from you in the square. His holds an unfinished one.

A fragment is not merely evidence that the Voyd exists.

It is evidence against the cat who carried it.

The orange projection turns its head and speaks your name in your own voice.

Every rooftop cat in Faelspire falls silent.

The silence is worse than the screaming.

The stray looks up.

“They know which cat to ask next.”
