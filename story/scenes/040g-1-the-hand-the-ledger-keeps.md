# The Hand the Ledger Keeps

Your forepaw is on the sheet's edge.

You do not think about it. Thinking is the square, and the square is a filed frame three paces away. The lever is a thing that moves when a paw moves it.

You pull.

The seam along the door's frame waits, then the sheet moves. The square comes up out of the black: your head beginning to turn, the fragment by your paw, the look-away, held to the last heartbeat and then let go. The frame finishes because you finish it, and as the last of it settles the seam loosens its aim.

It has been aimed at you since the square. The aim is gone. The pale light is on the edge of the sheet, and the edge is under your paw, and the paw is yours, and the paw does not come off.

You tried to take it off. It did not come. The method does not aim at the hand that moves it, and the hand that moves it is the one that will move it again. The method will need a paw when it works, and the paw will move, and you will not be able to put it down.

The door is open. It was never closed, and it will stay open while the hand keeps working.

P'taxx's face is on the cold stone at the desk. His paws are flat, his ears are back. He does not look at you, and there is nothing to look at him with now.

You have carried the city's corrections for two years. Sealed scrolls in a satchel that is never opened twice: the names the palace files, the names it should not have. When a name was wrong you carried the correction to the door that fixed it, and you never opened the seal twice, and you never told anyone which names you had held. This room keeps the same way, only it never signs the corrections out.

The record about you is open under your other paw. It will finish when the method works it, and the method is working, and the method is your paw. You will not hand it to the hand the method keeps. The hand is your paw.

You want him out of this room. The door is open, and he cannot follow you out while his face is on the stone. You will find the way to get him out. Not the way the record closes. Not the way the method works. Your way, the way you have always carried things: open, unfinished, until someone signs them.
