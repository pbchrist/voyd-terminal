# THE VOYD: A COMPLETE MYTHOGRAPHY

*Gate of Nyandor Canon Reference* *Compiled from The Furbidden Voyd (Book 1) and The Divergent Path (Book 2)* *Approved by Patrick Christell as pure canon.*

---

## I. WHAT THE VOYD IS, AT ITS MOST PRIMAL LEVEL

Before the first word is spoken about Voyd portals, Voyd magic, or the Great Severing, one must understand what the Voyd *is*, which is an entirely different order of question.

The Voyd is not a place. It is not a dimension in any navigable sense. It is **the darkness that predates creation itself** — the silence that existed before Light was even a word to be forgotten. C.A.T. opens Book 2 with the closest thing the series has to a definition: *"This was the blackness before the first star, the deep and patient quiet that existed before Light was even a word to be forgotten."*

The Voyd held the potential to birth a Mewniverse but felt no impulse to create. It once dreamed of light — a fleeting, internal glimmer that was a template for something greater — but it had no memory to hold the dream, so it forgot. It forgot warmth. It forgot form. It forgot sound. It forgot it was alone, because to register loneliness requires the concept of an Other. It simply *was*: a silent, patient vessel that had been waiting for a story it no longer remembered how to begin.

Mother Ertree, who reached the Voyd through a meditation deeper than any cat has gone, describes it as *"the darkness before Creation"* and *"the darkness that has never known light."* She spent what felt like aeons there — though in physical time it was a moment — and emerged fundamentally altered, her pupils blown wide, shaking with what she witnessed. Even Ertree, who has lived longer and traveled deeper into Stillness than nearly any cat alive, came back broken. The Voyd does that. It is cosmically too large for a finite consciousness to contain.

At its metaphysical root, the Voyd is the **negative space inside Leoran's mind**: the infinite un-written portions of the grand tale. Leoran, the Creator of All, contains within his consciousness every possible timeline — all outcomes past, present, and future, every choice taken and not taken, by every cat, across all time, and all the intersections between those choices. That totality is *Leoran's mind.* The Voyd is what that mind rests *in.* It is the darkness between the timestreams, the substrate under the story. A Voyd portal is, in the most literal sense, a hole punched into the floor of reality, opening into the ocean below the ocean.

## II. THE METAPHYSICS OF VOYD MAGIC: HOW IT DIFFERS FROM VOREATH

This distinction is critical and is the source of every catastrophe in the series.

Conventional magic — Voreath — draws from nature. It channels the latent forces of the Mewniverse, the wellspring energies that bleed through the Nyand. It is, at base, borrowing. It flows from Leoran. It works with the existing fabric of reality.

Voyd magic does something else entirely. As Ertree puts it: *"Unlike traditional magic, which channels the latent forces of nature, Voyd magic is different. It doesn't draw from the world, it writes it."*

It is a **simulacrum of Leoran's fatemaking.** Leoran's act of creation collapses infinite possibility into a single chosen outcome — he takes the limitless branching potential of all possible realities and says *this one.* Voyd magic does the same thing, except a mortal cat is holding the pen. By opening a Voyd portal — a window into Leoran's mind where all possible outcomes exist simultaneously — a caster can reach into the web of all possible timelines and extract a specific outcome, re-weaving the present around it.

The key mechanism: *time flows but one way.* A Voyd portal does not send you backwards. It allows you to reach *back* to any pivotal moment and change it, so that the present is *rewoven* from that point forward. The fabric of what is becomes the fabric of what you willed it to be. Everyone and everything associated with that outcome changes with the decision — they simply do not know, because for them, there was no other timeline.

To undo a Voyd-wrought change, one must do the opposite: collapse the fixed outcome *back* into possibility. Unchoose it. Release the clenched fist of intention and let the threads scatter back into potential. This is the mechanism of closing a portal — but as Ertree notes grimly, if the portal has been fed by more than one will, only the dominant will can release it. Or: a perfectly synchronized simultaneous release from all who contributed to it. And that is assuming the portal hasn't become powerful enough to resist entirely.

## III. THE PHYSICAL APPEARANCE AND BEHAVIOR OF AN OPEN VOYD PORTAL

The portal — referred to by Orachys and the Crown as "the Event" — has a distinctive, terrifying, and utterly alien appearance.

It is a **perfect circle.** Not approximately circular, not an irregular aperture, but geometrically perfect — as if punched into reality by a divine compass. Its color is not black in the ordinary sense. Firetooth, a Council member who sees it for the first time, notes it perfectly: *"The utter blackness of it. It doesn't reflect light — it devours it."* The disc is blacker than the surrounding darkness by an order that makes the darkness around it appear luminous. When Sory'n first conjures it, it is marble-sized. By the time her father sees it, it is the size of a dinner charger. Within fifteen hours, it doubles in diameter. It doubles again, and again.

Its edges, the event horizon, shimmer with a faint gray mist, tendrils of energy that trail away from the perimeter. When objects are cast into it — infusibles, an astrolabe, pebbles, a measuring stick — those gray tendrils flash the color of whatever Voreath-charged object just entered, then subside. It devours without return. Nothing thrown in comes back out in its original form. The astrolabe Orachys feeds it comes back as a molten spindle of lava that bores through solid rock on contact.

The portal moves. This is one of the first things that terrifies Sory'n. She conjures it in one spot on the outcropping; the next morning it has shifted. It has preferences, in the way a tide has preferences. It does not stay where it is put.

It produces **magnetism.** This is the detail that escalates from curiosity to existential threat. From a distance, it is subtle: a whisker drawn toward it, fur standing on end, a wildflower straining sideways with no wind. Closer, pebbles accelerate toward it and vanish. Closer still, a measuring stick jerks from your paws and disappears. When it grows large enough, the radius of its magnetic field expands commensurately — and exponentially. By the climax of Book 2, its pull is tearing grass flat against rock, splintering the Ashai's branches, peeling the corpses from their fallen armor and spiraling them into a grinding vortex. A dropped pike animates and launches itself like a javelin. The entire clearing unmakes itself. *The pull intensified.* Flimsy tents become frantic kites on an invisible wind.

It also produces **a pressure**, described as a crushing wave of pure potential that makes the air thick and hard to breathe. Standing near an active, growing portal is a physical ordeal. It radiates something that is the opposite of channeled Voreath — raw, untamed, alien current that the body fights and the trained magician's instincts recoil from.

It is completely, absolutely, profoundly **silent.** No sound comes from it. Objects vanish into it without so much as a whisper. Its silence is part of what makes it so unnatural. *A perfect circle of absolute, light-devouring black, its edges shimmering with a faint, grey mist.*

And it is **seductive.** Multiple characters describe a pull that is not only physical but psychological. Sory'n gives it a deep sniff and notes the air it carries holds no scent at all — a void of olfactory information in a world of cats whose entire sense-architecture is scent-based. The sight of its *"perfect, silent emptiness was a mystery that seemed to pull at her thoughts, its darkness promising a quiet so profound it felt like the ultimate truth."* Even a drawing of a Voyd portal, three thousand years old, carries an echo potent enough that P'taxx slaps his niece's paw away from the page and brushes the charcoal dust off it with concern.

## IV. THE HISTORY: DENIDRATA SOL AND THE FIRST GREAT SEVERING

Three thousand years before Sory'n was born, in the last years of the First Era of Nyandorian civilization, the world had a different relationship with magic. There were no prohibitions. Cats of all stripes practiced Voreath freely. The Magical Guild of Voreath existed solely to catalog advancements in the field. Magic was not a privilege; it was a birthright.

Denidrata Sol was the last Archmage of the First Era — a mage of extraordinary power who served King Registus, a tyrant who had embroiled Nyandor in a war against the Tol Dominion. By Sol's own diary, three years into that war, it became clear to him that the war was unwinnable. Sol was brilliant, and Sol was twisted, and Sol was desperate. He sought the Voyd portal as a weapon: to create a timeline where King Registus had never declared war.

According to Ertree's account of his diary: he believed, for a time, that he had succeeded. A timeline without the war did appear to stabilize. But then the collateral changes began arriving. His wife came home one night and did not recognize him. Sol began trying to repair the fractures — to wish away each unintended consequence with another portal invocation. And here is where the catastrophe engine ignited.

With each invocation, the portal grew more powerful. The more energy he fed it, the more demanding his intentions became. The more specific his wishes, paradoxically, the more chaotic the timeline changes that resulted. He was, in Ertree's framing, trying to tie off errant story threads with a pair of scissors while the garment was already unraveling — and every snip created three new loose ends.

The portal grew. And grew. Until it swallowed the entire timeline.

**THE GREAT SEVERING.**

The liturgical account in the Unified Vulgate, Book of the Sundering:

*"Once came the Great Severing, and with it, ash and silence. When the heavens wept fire, and the very bones of Nyand groaned in anguish. When the burning ceased and the dust lay still upon the land, that which was known had been unmade. The memory of the First Era dissolved like breath on glass. Then did the cats wander in shadow, forgetting even the shape of speech, the weaving of thought, and the binding of Voreath, once held in the hollow of their paws. All was to be learned anew, scraped from silence and shadow, guided only by the faintest echo of Leoran's mercy and holy breath.* *— The Unified Vulgate, Book of the Sundering, Chapter Three, Verses One to Six."*

The Guild's official narrative pins the blame on *common* magicians' selfish acts angering Leoran, who withdrew magical gifts as divine punishment. The truth, known to Ertree and to whoever stole Sol's diary from the Archives vault, is different: corruption festered not at the common level but at the Ziggurat's peak. Sol weaponized magic for a tyrant. He found the Voyd. He used it. And it ate the world.

After the Severing, the survivors purged all First Era spell guides. Every combination of sacred pawsitions that could produce magic was destroyed. Sol's diary was sealed in a vault beneath the Archives, guarded fifteen-hour shifts by soldiers, accessible only to the Archmage and to those with his bloodline's clearance. And critically — Sol never recorded *how* to produce a Voyd portal. Only what it looked like after it existed. What your father is doing, Ertree tells Sory'n, is like trying to pick a lock with infinite tumblers.

## V. THE OBSESSION VECTOR: HOW THE VOYD RECRUITS ITS CASTERS

Here is where the Voyd stops being merely a catastrophic magical phenomenon and reveals itself as something closer to an **active intelligence.**

C.A.T. names it plainly, rolling a die on his cave table: *"The Voyd doesn't speak in metaphor. It employs a different language. The language of fate."*

The Voyd plants lures. It leaves synchronicities. It reaches across timelines and leaves pawprints that its intended caster interprets as fate. In Sory'n's story: the orange cat she followed without knowing why, pulled to P'taxx's shop without a map or directions. The Strays she met, who happened to know about wellsprings — which happened to be the exact piece of knowledge she was missing to make the conjuration work. The moment she found herself standing at the exact configuration of rock and warped Ashai that Denidrata Sol had sketched three thousand years ago. *"The exact configuration. The very same spot."*

Each of these signposts, interpreted by Sory'n as the fateful paw of Leoran guiding her, were instead — or simultaneously — the Voyd calling out to her through its strange magnetism. And the more the Voyd has its say, the stronger it gets. *"In this manner, the story ceases to be about her… and quietly, inexorably, becomes about it."*

More terrifying: Mother Ertree's vision from the depths of Stillness suggests this is not particular to Sory'n. It may be particular to **cats.** Cats are defined by curiosity — it is their deepest, most primal drive. A cat awakened to Voreath will, if they dig deeply enough into the mystery of the Great Severing, inevitably stumble onto knowledge of the Voyd. And once they understand what it can do, they become obsessed. Orachys tried and failed 694,473 times before Sory'n conjured it at the wellspring. The Felixia Street hucksters knew about it. The Voyd Adherents, a cult that springs up around the open Event in Book 2 and calls it "the Eye of Rakas," knew about it. Knowledge seeps. Every era has its obsessives. And Ertree's vision from the depths of the Voyd suggests that *the most curious cat in any given timeline eventually uncovers Voyd magic and triggers another Great Severing.* Time and again. Cycle after cycle.

The seed of destruction is not in the Voyd. It is in the cats.

## VI. THE WELLSPRING CONDITION: HOW A VOYD PORTAL IS ACTUALLY CONJURED

There are two things Orachys had, after 694,473 attempts, that Sory'n had and he did not: the right location, and the right state.

A Voyd portal cannot be conjured in a laboratory. Orachys could produce a marble-sized black singularity — a prototype, a flicker — in the controlled conditions of his workroom, using painstaking empirical variations of paired pawsition diagrams. Each attempt produced a singularity that lasted a fraction of a second before collapsing with an audible *snap.* He cataloged every failure with an X and a number. He had been doing this for half his adult life.

What he was missing was the wellspring.

A wellspring is a place where magical energy bleeds through the Nyand — a natural concentration point in the living field of Leoran's creation. Ertree articulates the key principle: *"In a place with a vibrant, flowing wellspring, you barely need pawsitions. They just happen. Source energy puppeteers your paws into place. All Sol needed was nature and intention."*

At the wellspring beneath the rock outcropping — the very outcropping in Sol's sketch, with the violently twisted Ashai tree warped by three thousand years of wellspring energy — Sory'n enters the deepest Stillness of her life. She dissolves. There is no Sory'n, no agenda, no self — only a drop in an ocean of intelligent consciousness. Her paws begin moving of their own accord, tracing invisible rails that had been etched into existence aeons before her birth. She speaks an invocation not as a prayer but as a surrender, listing her intentions honestly: to redress wrongs done to Ertree's clowder, to unmake the Guild, to restore the truth of the common spark to all cats.

And she falls into the endless black Voyd.

What she finds inside is the raw material of the Mewniverse: an infinite, living, neuronal web of glowing filaments pulsing in unidirectional flow. Timestreams. Incomprehensible in scale. She is not merely looking at them — she is *in* them, and they are *her.* She can zip along a single filament and experience a whole potential life — a hearth, Doye brushing against her, years of love — or she can pull back and hurtle toward a different stream and experience another lifetime entire, or she can shoot to a parallel strand and feel what her life would have been had she succeeded at the Proving Ground. She is inside the mind of Leoran, navigating the full architecture of all possible selves and all possible histories.

The conjuration leaves a physical trace: the wellspring-fed Ashai's branches warp and torque into the same violently spiraled configuration captured in Sol's three-thousand-year-old sketch. The wellspring itself bears the mark of the event that took place over it.

## VII. GROWTH, FEEDING, AND THE DOMINANT WILL

The portal, once open, does not stay the size it was conjured. It grows. By Sory'n's measurement, it is doubling in diameter every fifteen hours. The radius of magnetic effect tracks in an inverse-square relationship with field decay, increasing proportionally with projected volume growth. Orachys runs the calculation and realizes they are dealing with an exponential growth curve.

The portal also *feeds* on will. Every invocation strengthens it. Every consciousness that interacts with it, every intention directed at it, makes it more powerful and more resistant to closure. Orachys feeding infusibles into it charged with Voreath makes it stronger. The King's desire expressed through Orachys' invocations adds a third contributing will alongside Sory'n's original conjuration and Orachys' subsequent domination of it. The Voyd Adherents' organized worship around the Event in the clearing — their chanting, their synchronized reverence — is raw fuel. Every cat who approaches with awe or intention is feeding it.

This creates the governing rule of closure: *only the dominant will can release it.* The cat with the deepest, most sustained connection to the portal is its current master. At the climax of Book 2, that is Orachys, who has been working with it daily, feeding it his singular obsessive focus for weeks. His psychological grip on it is expressed in the Null State as a battering ram of pure ambition: *By my will, this gate will remain! The power of the Voyd is MY CREATION. The power of the Voyd is MINE ALONE!*

The alternative to the dominant will releasing it: a perfectly synchronized simultaneous release from every contributing will. Sory'n, Orachys, the King. All three unchoosing at the same moment. But the King does not know what he contributed. And Orachys has no intention of releasing anything.

The third route — the one the clowder attempts at the climax — is a unified, collective will powerful enough to override the dominant will through sheer coordinated force: Sory'n, Leba, Doye, and Chartru'el, each bringing their individual light (the gravitational brown of Leba's rootedness, the electric gold of Doye's curiosity, the blooming orange of Chartru'el's warmth, and Sory'n's crystalline silver of precision and pride), weaving them into a constellation of four against Orachys' psychic roar.

The portal, caught in the tug of war, does not collapse cleanly. It tears. Cracks shot from top to bottom. Blinding internal light poured through the seams. Then spiderweb fractures. Then violent, vibrating shards.

The Voyd does not go quietly.

## VIII. THE CYCLICAL THEORY: THE VOYD'S DEEPEST HORROR

Ertree's vision in the depths of Stillness discloses the series' central existential terror, and it is worth spelling out precisely.

She goes deeper into Stillness than she has ever gone. Past abstraction. Past dissolution. She reaches *the darkness before Creation.* And there she sees: the rock outcropping Sol drew. The warped Ashai. Robed cats in terrified debate. She sees something else, something she can barely bring herself to speak, that she implies about the future.

Then she articulates the theoretical framework that makes it worse: What if the Great Severing was not a singular historical event? What if it was an *iteration*? What if the most curious cat in any given timeline — driven by the deepest feline instinct — always, inevitably, uncovers Voyd magic? What if this has happened *countless times before,* and there was never anything anyone could do to stop it?

*"What if our entire hisstory is just a cycle of Great Severings? Cat kind destroyed time and time again, curiosity forever leading us by the nose to inevitable ruin?"*

The implication: the Voyd is not the enemy. The Voyd is the environment. The enemy is the combination of feline nature (curiosity is inextinguishable) and the Voyd's own recruitment mechanism, which exploits that nature. Every timeline produces its Denidrata Sol. Every timeline produces its Orachys. And perhaps, every timeline produces its Sory'n — the one who *opens* it for reasons she believes are pure, not understanding that purity of intention has never once protected the world from the Voyd's appetite.

C.A.T. confirms this reading directly. He names his tally: *"I've run this story six hundred ninety-four thousand, four hundred and seventy-three times, and the outcome never wavers. Mewniverse destroyed. Darkness forever."*

That number — 694,473 — is not a coincidence. It is the exact number of failed attempts Orachys recorded in his laboratory notebook before Sory'n succeeded. The Voyd has been trying to be opened for as long as Orachys has been trying to open it. C.A.T. has been running the story for exactly as many iterations as Orachys has been running his experiment. The numbers are the same because they *are* the same thing: the Voyd's own persistence, its patience, its eternal circling back to the same experiment. Orachys is not obsessed. He *is the obsession.* He is the Voyd's instrument, and he doesn't know it.

## IX. WHO IS CHASING IT, WHEN, AND WHY

The Voyd has no shortage of pursuers. They are layered across the series in escalating proximity to the thing.

**Orachys of Tol.** The most dangerous, because the most capable. He has dedicated half his adult life — 694,473 attempts spanning decades — to creating a Voyd portal. His stated purpose to the King: a military advantage against Irlandia. His stated purpose to Sory'n: pure research, the greatest discovery in the history of magic. His real motivation, glimpsed in moments of honesty: he wants to bend spacetime to feline will. He tells Sory'n plainly that when you have the means to alter reality to your specifications, *no particular outcome matters.* That sentence is the definition of a god complex. Once Sory'n opens it and he wrests control, his focus is not on closing it; it is on learning to direct it with absolute precision and scaling up.

**King Greytail IV.** Incidental and almost comically mundane in his motivation. He wants to solve the Irlandian naval blockade at the Foreleg. He wants a political win. He does not understand what he is asking to use. He provides the Royal Writ of Emergency Action that shields Orachys from the Guild's attempts to shut the research down, and in doing so makes himself a contributing will to the portal's power. He is the patron of the apocalypse through pure institutional incompetence and short-sightedness.

**Mother Ertree.** She wants the portal for the opposite of Orachys: liberation. Her sworn mission is to awaken all cats to their magical heritage. With a Voyd portal, she could reach back to the moment the Edict of Concealment was ratified and create a timeline where it never was — a world where no kitten was ever drowned for being born with the spark, where magic was never monopolized by the powerful. The fact that Sol's version of the same impulse (a noble goal, stop a war) destroyed the world does not seem to stop her from being tempted. Ertree is terrifyingly self-aware about this. She describes the thought arriving, the darkness entering her eyes, her claws digging into stone as she fights it. She steps back. But she came *very* close.

**M'okto Spottedlegs.** The Guild Guardian's interest in the Voyd is purely suppressive. He knows. He has always known. He personally ensured the scrubbing of Nyandic current references from the Nekonomicon so that no future researcher could stumble on the missing piece. He maintains the vault beneath the Archives where Sol's diary is locked. He has begged Orachys to stop, invoked the Great Severing directly, called it the highest crime against the Guild, the Crown, and the plane of reality. His motivation is not cruelty. It is the specific, well-informed terror of a historian who knows exactly what the endgame looks like: *"The entire world was destroyed."*

**The Voyd Adherents.** A cult that springs up organically around the open Event in Book 2, calling it "the Eye of Rakas" and organizing worship around it. They see what everyone else sees and conclude it is sacred rather than catastrophic. They reinforce and extend the portal's power with their reverence. They are the most literal of the Voyd's recruits — ordinary cats who walked close enough to feel its pull and decided it was calling them home.

**The Hornblower reporters, Cattinson and Maidencoat Spur.** Not chasers, but witnesses. They have their own scoop to protect and questions to answer. Their published report — speculating that the Event is connected to the Great Severing — sows mass panic in Faelspire and forces the King's hand into a public statement. They are the accidental press agents of the apocalypse.

**The clowder of four (Sory'n, Leba, Doye, Chartru'el).** They are not chasing the Voyd so much as they are chasing the possibility of closing it before it finishes what Orachys started. Sory'n is the original caster, the one with the constitutional opening connection, the one Ertree believes must be the agent of its closure. The clowder's closing attempt in the final confrontation is the series' first genuine thesis test: whether four young cats, unified, can overpower a fully obsessed, dominant-will archmage and the accumulated energy of weeks of feeding.

**The Voyd itself.** This is not metaphor. C.A.T. makes it explicit. The Voyd chooses. The Voyd lures. The Voyd left synchronicities for Sory'n across multiple timelines — *"these signposts, these lures, reach across timelines and leave their pawprints."* The orange cat she followed. The Strays and their wellspring knowledge. The path to the exact spot Sol drew. Every story beat that Sory'n read as the fateful paw of Leoran was the Voyd speaking its preferred language: fate. And the more it speaks, the more power it gathers. The Voyd is hunting for a caster the way a lock hunts for the right key. When it found Sory'n, it had found what it always finds — the most willing, the most desperate, the most genuine cat in the room. It did not want Orachys, the cynic. It wanted the believer.

## X. THE QUESTION C.A.T. CANNOT ANSWER

Six hundred ninety-four thousand, four hundred and seventy-three times, the story ends the same way.

The Mewniverse is destroyed.

Darkness forever.

C.A.T. rolls his dice. A feline ghost. A forked path. An ouroboros. And a human hand.

*"Just as a game is no fun to play alone, a tale has no power if there's no one to hear it. That may be what this one has always lacked. Not another choice by its characters, but the hopes of a human mind pressing in from the outside. It could be that the only way out of this terrible loop isn't another choice by a character at all. Purrhaps it's a choice you make."*

The Voyd is the darkness that preceded creation. The portal is the wound in creation's side. And every cat who has ever tried to fix that wound with their own will — however righteous, however brilliant, however well-intentioned — has only made it larger.

The only thing that has never been tried, across 694,473 iterations, is a reader choosing differently.

That is the lock. The Voyd has been the key all along.

## ADDENDUM B: SOL'S DIARY — CHAIN OF CUSTODY

- Original: written by Denidrata Sol, First Era. Believed lost in the Great Severing.  
- A reconstruction — "a copy of a copy, passed down through… best we don't get into that" (P'taxx) — was held in a locked vault beneath the Guild Archives, accessible only by the Archmage and bloodline-cleared archivists.  
- Orachys of Tol stole it. He used it not to learn *how* to open a portal (Sol never recorded that), but to understand the only surviving record of what Voyd magic looked like in practice.  
- Doye Spottedlegs discovered the vault copy had been replaced with a blank-paged forgery — same cracked leather cover, fresh parchment inside, no First Era cat-scratch. The theft was designed to look undisturbed.  
- The original Sol sketch — depicting the black orb above the rock outcropping, the twisted Ashai, the terrified robed cats — was reproduced in the laboratory. Sory'n and Ertree later burned it. The charcoal strokes of the circular Voyd portal itself refused to burn; they glowed orange in the flame after all surrounding parchment had turned to cinders, and the shape seared into Sory'n's mind permanently.

## ADDENDUM C: THE VOYD IN DREAMS AND VISIONS

The Voyd communicates outside waking experience, and this appears to be deliberate.

Sory'n's first encounter with the portal — before she conjures it — comes in a dream: a flash of a perfect, black disc, darker than darkness, and a cold, gut-wrenching certainty that it was waiting for her. She does not yet know what it is. She wakes terrified.

Ertree accesses the Voyd through deep Stillness meditation, not conjuration. She touches the darkness before creation, witnesses Sol's original site across three thousand years of time, and comes back changed — eyes blown wide, voice brittle, and in possession of a vision she can barely speak aloud. Her implication that she witnessed something about *the future* at the original conjuration site is left open.

The portal also appears in Sory'n's consciousness during the conjuration itself as an immersive, total-submersion experience — not a vision but direct contact: the timestream web fills her entire field of awareness, she navigates it bodily (or the consciousness-equivalent of bodily), and the weight of every unchosen life crashes into her. It is not symbolic. It is real. What she experiences in the Voyd during conjuration has emotional force equivalent to having actually lived the events.

## ADDENDUM D: THE VOYD AND WELLSPRINGS — PHYSICAL RELATIONSHIP

The wellspring beneath the rock outcropping is not merely a condition for conjuration. It has a persistent, visible, physical relationship with the Voyd's presence.

The Ashai tree above the wellspring is warped. Its trunk twists into a tight spiral; its branches are bent at sharp, wrong angles — a violent torquing that would not occur in any natural growth pattern. This is three thousand years of wellspring energy responding to or recording the fact that Denidrata Sol opened a Voyd portal at this exact location. The tree is a scar.

When Sory'n touches the Ashai's trunk, the vibrations of the wellspring course through her immediately. The tree functions as a conductor or antenna — not for Voreath, but for the wellspring energy that makes Voyd conjuration possible. It amplifies the connection between the caster and the substrate below the substrate.

After Sory'n's conjuration, the tree's warping matches Sol's sketch exactly, down to the specific angle of each branch. The wellspring records what happens above it. The site is now, for the second time in three thousand years, marked.

## ADDENDUM F: PROHIBITED DISTORTIONS — WHAT THE VOYD IS NOT

These are not matters of interpretation. They are canon errors.

- The Voyd is **not** simply a dark dimension you can visit or travel through.  
- Voyd magic is **not** more powerful Voreath. It is categorically different in kind — it writes reality rather than borrowing from it.  
- A Voyd portal does **not** send the caster backward in time. Time flows one way. The portal reaches back to change the present, not relocate the caster.  
- The Great Severing was **not** Leoran's punishment of common cats for selfish acts. That is Guild propaganda. The Severing was caused by Sol, at the Ziggurat's peak, serving a tyrant.  
- Orachys is **not** simply an ambitious mage who went bad. He is the Voyd's instrument across 694,473 iterations. The obsession is not his; it is the Voyd's, operating through him.  
- The Voyd is **not** neutral. It recruits. It lures. It leaves synchronicities across timelines. It acts.  
- Wellspring locations are **not** interchangeable. The specific outcropping in the Eastern Forest, where Sol conjured the original portal, is the locus. The warped Ashai above the wellspring is both record and conductor.
